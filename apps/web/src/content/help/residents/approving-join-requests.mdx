When residents use the self-service **Join community** flow, they land in the join-requests queue. Admins review and act.

## The queue

Open `/admin/join-requests` to see every pending request with:

- The resident's name, email, and optional phone
- The unit they claim to live in or own
- Their declared role (Owner, Tenant, etc.)
- Any supporting evidence they attached
- Submission timestamp

Sort by age to keep the queue fresh — old requests often mean a resident is frustrated.

## Reviewing a request

<StepByStep>
<Step title="Open the request">
Click any row to see the full submission.
</Step>

<Step title="Verify the unit claim">
Cross-check the claimed unit number against your ownership and tenant records. Most disputes boil down to a unit that doesn't match records.
</Step>

<Step title="Verify role">
Owners should appear on your title or ownership list. Tenants should have a lease on file or be named by the owner. If neither checks out, ask for proof before approving.
</Step>

<Step title="Approve">
Click **Approve**. The resident gets an email, and they're added to the roster with their declared role.
</Step>

<Step title="Or deny with a reason">
Click **Deny** and write a short reason (e.g., "Unit number doesn't match our records. Please confirm with your closing paperwork and resubmit."). The resident sees the reason and can resubmit.
</Step>
</StepByStep>

## When in doubt

<Callout type="warning" title="Default to a temporary role">
If you're unsure whether a requester is really an owner or a tenant, approve as Tenant (lower permissions) and ask them to provide documentation. It's easier to elevate later than to take permissions back.
</Callout>

## Common patterns

- **Tenant claiming owner role** — very common. Tenants sometimes pick Owner because they assume it means "person living in unit." Approve as Tenant and message them.
- **Former owner whose name is still on a prior deed** — if they sold, they're no longer eligible. Deny with "ownership has transferred per our records" and ask them to clarify if that's wrong.
- **Unit typos** — common for units with leading zeros or letters. Approve if the resident's identity is clear, and fix the unit number on their profile.

## Bulk triage

During onboarding for a new community, you may have dozens of pending requests. Work them in order of unit to group similar cases, or by age to clear stale ones first. There's no bulk-approve button by design — each request deserves a quick human look.

## Audit trail

Every approval and denial is captured in the compliance audit log with the admin's name and the reason. If a resident later disputes that they were denied or delayed, you have a timestamped record.
