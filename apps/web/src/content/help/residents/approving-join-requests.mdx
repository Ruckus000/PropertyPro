When residents use the self-service **Join community** flow, their request lands in one of two admin queues. Both need a human review — approve to grant access, or deny with a reason.

## Which queue applies?

PropertyPro maintains two separate review surfaces. Most communities use one or both depending on onboarding path:

| Queue | Where admins act | What you see |
| ----- | ---------------- | ------------ |
| **Join requests** | `/admin/join-requests` | Unit identifier, declared role (Owner or Tenant), submission date |
| **Access requests** | **Residents** → scroll to **Access Requests** (`/communities/[id]/residents`) | Full name, email, claimed unit (with match indicator), declared role, submission date |

**Join requests** come from the self-service `/account/join-community` flow and are stored as `community_join_requests`.

**Access requests** appear on the Residents page when someone requests access through that path (for example, after an invite or a community-specific access link). They carry richer identity fields and show whether the claimed unit matches your roster.

If you're unsure which queue a resident used, search both surfaces — the same person should not appear in both, but during onboarding you may see traffic in each.

## The join-requests queue

Open `/admin/join-requests` to see every pending join request with:

- The unit identifier the resident entered
- Their declared role (Owner or Tenant)
- Submission timestamp

There are no sort controls — work through requests in the order shown.

## The access-requests queue

Open **Residents** for the community and scroll to **Access Requests**. Each card shows:

- Requester's name and email
- Claimed unit (with a match indicator when the unit exists on your roster)
- Declared role (Owner or Tenant)
- Submission date

Use **Approve** or **Deny** on each card. Denials prompt for a short reason the requester sees in an in-app notification.

## Reviewing a request (either queue)

The verification steps are the same whether the request appears under **Join requests** or **Access requests**:

<StepByStep>
<Step title="Verify the unit claim">
Cross-check the claimed unit number against your ownership and tenant records. On the access-requests queue, use the unit match indicator as a hint — but still confirm against your source records. Most disputes boil down to a unit that doesn't match records.
</Step>

<Step title="Verify role">
Owners should appear on your title or ownership list. Tenants should have a lease on file or be named by the owner. If neither checks out, deny and ask for proof before approving.
</Step>

<Step title="Approve">
Click **Approve** on the request card. The resident receives an in-app notification and is added to the roster with their declared role.
</Step>

<Step title="Or deny with a reason">
Click **Deny**, enter a short reason (e.g., "Unit number doesn't match our records. Please confirm with your closing paperwork and resubmit."), and confirm. The resident sees the reason in an in-app notification.
</Step>
</StepByStep>

## When in doubt

<Callout type="warning" title="Deny and ask them to resubmit">
If you're unsure whether a requester is really an owner or a tenant, deny with a note asking them to resubmit as the correct type. Approval grants the declared role — there is no way to change it later in the UI.
</Callout>

## Common patterns

- **Tenant claiming owner role** — very common. Tenants sometimes pick Owner because they assume it means "person living in unit." Approve as Tenant only if you're confident, or deny and ask them to resubmit as Tenant.
- **Former owner whose name is still on a prior deed** — if they sold, they're no longer eligible. Deny with "ownership has transferred per our records" and ask them to clarify if that's wrong.
- **Unit typos** — common for units with leading zeros or letters. If the resident's identity is clear but the unit is wrong, deny and ask them to resubmit with the correct unit.

## Bulk triage

During onboarding for a new community, you may have dozens of pending requests. Work them in order of unit to group similar cases, or by age to clear stale ones first. There's no bulk-approve button by design — each request deserves a quick human look.

## Audit trail

Every approval and denial is captured in the compliance audit log with the admin's name and the reason. If a resident later disputes that they were denied or delayed, you have a timestamped record.
