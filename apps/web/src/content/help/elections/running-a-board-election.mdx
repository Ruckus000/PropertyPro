A board election in PropertyPro follows the statutory process for Florida condos (§718.112(2)(d), §718.128 for e-voting) and HOAs (§720.306(9)). Every step has timing rules — this guide walks the full flow.

<Callout type="florida-statute" title="Two-step notice">
Condo elections require a two-step notice: first notice goes out 60 days before the election (soliciting candidates); second notice goes out 14 days before with the final ballot. Prepare and send both notices outside PropertyPro (for example, via Announcements or Documents). PropertyPro does not generate, draft, or send election notices, and notice deadlines are not tracked in the app.
</Callout>

## Before you start

Have these ready:

- The election date (typically at the annual meeting)
- The eligible-voter snapshot date — usually a few days before the election
- Your association's attorney's contact (e-voting often requires attorney sign-off before first election)
- Governing documents confirming the number of seats and term lengths

## Phase 1 — First notice (60 days before)

<StepByStep>
<Step title="Open the election">
Open **Board → Elections**. PropertyPro has no UI to create elections — they appear in the list once set up. From the election detail, admins can use **Snapshot Eligibility**, **Open Election**, **Close Election**, **Certify Results**, and **Cancel Election**.
</Step>

<Step title="Send the candidate-solicitation notice">
Prepare and send the first notice outside PropertyPro. Candidates must submit written intent at least 40 days before the election — about 20 days after the first notice goes out.
</Step>

<Step title="Prepare candidate materials">
Add each candidate's name and a short description. Control display order yourself — candidates cannot upload bios in PropertyPro.
</Step>
</StepByStep>

## Phase 2 — Second notice and ballot (14 days before)

<StepByStep>
<Step title="Close candidacy">
Candidacy closes 40 days before the election per statute. Maintain the slate manually — PropertyPro does not auto-close candidate intake.
</Step>

<Step title="Snapshot eligibility">
On or shortly before the notice date, take the eligibility snapshot. Each unit entitled to vote is captured — delinquent owners may lose voting rights per your bylaws.
</Step>

<Step title="Send the second notice">
The second notice includes the final ballot, candidate bios, and voting instructions (both e-voting and paper). Sent 14 days before the election.
</Step>
</StepByStep>

## Phase 3 — Voting window

<Callout type="florida-statute" title="E-voting requires opt-in">
Under §718.128, each unit owner must opt in to electronic voting separately — the association cannot enroll owners automatically. Paper ballots must remain available for any owner who prefers them.
</Callout>

During the voting window, owners can:

- Submit an e-ballot through their PropertyPro account (one ballot per unit, not per user)
- Request or submit a paper ballot (handled entirely offline — counts are not entered into PropertyPro)
- Designate a proxy per the bylaws' proxy rules

## Phase 4 — Counting and certification

<StepByStep>
<Step title="Close the polls at the scheduled time">
Voting stops automatically after the election's close time if one was set. An authorized admin must still click **Close Election** to finalize the election status.
</Step>

<Step title="Count ballots">
E-ballots tally automatically in PropertyPro. Paper ballots are counted offline and are not entered into PropertyPro.
</Step>

<Step title="Quorum check">
The election is valid only if your quorum threshold is met. If not, the election fails and must be re-noticed.
</Step>

<Step title="Certify results">
An authorized admin clicks **Certify Results** in PropertyPro. Collect committee signatures separately (you can attach a results document when certifying).
</Step>

<Step title="Post results">
Record results in meeting minutes manually. Optionally link a results document — PropertyPro does not post results to minutes or update the compliance dashboard.
</Step>
</StepByStep>

## Contested elections

If results are contested:

- Preserve ballots (electronic trail is automatic; paper ballots go into the official records).
- Loop in the association attorney.
- Do not unilaterally recount or modify results without following bylaws.

## Common pitfalls

- **Late notice** — missing the 60-day or 14-day mark can invalidate the election. Set calendar reminders.
- **Eligibility disputes** — owners in collection or suspension may or may not be eligible; your bylaws decide.
- **Proxy abuse** — watch for one person holding too many proxies relative to what bylaws allow.
