Emergency broadcasts fan out community-wide via SMS and email. Use them for real emergencies only.

<Callout type="warning" title="Real emergencies only">
Emergency broadcasts disrupt residents at any time of day. Using them for non-emergencies (social events, policy reminders) damages trust and leads residents to ignore future alerts. Keep them reserved for life-safety, property-damage, or other genuinely urgent situations.
</Callout>

## Good reasons to broadcast

- **Severe weather** — hurricane warning, tornado watch, flash flood
- **Building emergencies** — fire, gas leak, major water main break
- **Immediate safety** — suspected intruder, medical emergency response
- **Major outages** — extended loss of water, electricity, elevator

## Not good reasons

- Scheduled power-washing that runs over
- A new gym rule
- Social event reminders
- Polls or feedback requests

Use a pinned announcement for those.

## Composing a broadcast

<StepByStep>
<Step title="Open Emergency Alerts">
Go to **Emergency Alerts**, then click **Send Emergency Alert** to start the four-step wizard: Template → Compose → Recipients → Confirm.
</Step>

<Step title="Choose a template or start from scratch">
Pick a built-in template or click **Start from scratch**, then enter severity, title, email body, and SMS body on the Compose step.
</Step>

<Step title="Select recipients and channels">
On the Recipients step, choose **All residents** or **Owners only**. Check **SMS** and/or **Email** under Channels.
</Step>

<Step title="Confirm and send">
On the Confirm step, check **I understand this will immediately alert…** then click **Send Emergency Alert**. A 10-second countdown starts — click **UNDO - Cancel Broadcast** if you need to stop it.
</Step>
</StepByStep>

## Tracking delivery

The broadcast page shows aggregate delivery telemetry — targeted, sent, delivered, and failed counts. Updates flow in from the SMS and email providers as they confirm delivery.

If a high number of failures appears, staff may need to follow up on those residents through another channel (phone, in-person) to make sure they got the information.

## Post-event follow-up

After the emergency is over:

1. Send a follow-up broadcast **or** a pinned announcement (depending on severity) with the all-clear, what happened, and any damage/impact report.
2. Review what worked: did the message reach everyone? Check delivery counts for failed sends.

## Pre-positioned templates

Before a stressful moment, review the built-in template picker on the first wizard step — hurricane, power outage, water main break, and others. Pick a template and adapt the compose step when the situation arises — much faster than writing from scratch while panicking.

## Audit trail

Every broadcast is captured in the compliance audit log. For claims or insurance purposes later, the sent broadcast, recipient counts, and delivery telemetry are timestamped evidence.

## Who should have the Emergency role

Limit emergency-broadcast permissions to the people who genuinely should send them — board president, CAM, site manager. An accidental broadcast is a trust-damaging event.
