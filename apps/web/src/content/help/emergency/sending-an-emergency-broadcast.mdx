Emergency broadcasts fan out community-wide through every channel you have — email, in-app, SMS where configured. They ignore quiet hours and notification preferences. Use them for real emergencies only.

<Callout type="warning" title="Real emergencies only">
Emergency broadcasts disrupt residents at any time of day. Using them for non-emergencies (social events, policy reminders) damages trust and leads residents to ignore future alerts. Keep them reserved for life-safety, property-damage, or other genuinely urgent situations.
</Callout>

## Good reasons to broadcast

- **Severe weather** — hurricane warning, tornado watch, flash flood
- **Building emergencies** — fire, gas leak, major water main break
- **Immediate safety** — suspected intruder, medical emergency response
- **Major outages** — extended loss of water, electricity, elevator

## Not good reasons

- Scheduled power-washing that runs over
- A new gym rule
- Social event reminders
- Polls or feedback requests

Use a pinned announcement for those.

## Composing a broadcast

<StepByStep>
<Step title="Open Emergency">
From the sidebar (admin only), click Emergency, then **New broadcast**.
</Step>

<Step title="State the situation">
Keep the subject concise — "Tornado warning: take shelter now." The body should answer: what's happening, what should residents do right now, and where to find updates.
</Step>

<Step title="Include any safety instructions">
Shelter locations, evacuation routes, emergency-contact numbers — anything that helps residents act.
</Step>

<Step title="Choose the audience">
By default, every community member. For building-specific issues, narrow to the affected building or wing.
</Step>

<Step title="Require acknowledgement (optional)">
For life-safety, require residents to tap Acknowledge. You'll see who has and hasn't — essential for headcounts during evacuation.
</Step>

<Step title="Send">
The broadcast goes out across every channel immediately. You cannot recall it — additional announcements can correct or update.
</Step>
</StepByStep>

## Tracking acknowledgements

If you required acknowledgement, the broadcast page shows a live count of acknowledgements, and you can see the list of residents who have and haven't. Staff can follow up on unaccounted residents physically.

## Post-event follow-up

After the emergency is over:

1. Send a follow-up broadcast **or** a pinned announcement (depending on severity) with the all-clear, what happened, and any damage/impact report.
2. Archive the emergency broadcast (closed events stay in the audit log but drop off the active list).
3. Review what worked: did the message reach everyone? Were acknowledgements fast?

## Pre-positioned templates

Before a stressful moment, create draft broadcasts for your most likely emergencies (hurricane, power outage, water main break). You can copy and adapt when the situation arises — much faster than writing from scratch while panicking.

## Audit trail

Every broadcast is captured in the compliance audit log. For claims or insurance purposes later, the sent broadcast, the recipient list, and the acknowledgement count are all timestamped evidence.

## Who should have the Emergency role

Limit emergency-broadcast permissions to the people who genuinely should send them — board president, CAM, site manager. An accidental broadcast is a trust-damaging event.
