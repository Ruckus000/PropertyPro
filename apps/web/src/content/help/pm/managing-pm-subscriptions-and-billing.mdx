PropertyPro bills per community. As a property manager, you typically manage billing centrally — you (or your company) pay for every community in your portfolio, then pass costs through per your management agreement.

## Plan tiers

Each plan tier unlocks a defined set of features:

- **Starter** — basic resident communication, document library, and meetings.
- **Standard** — adds compliance dashboard, finance, maintenance triage.
- **Pro** — adds e-sign, polls, advanced reporting, custom branding.
- **Enterprise** — everything in Pro plus custom-domain, premium support, SLA.

Specific feature mappings are documented at the plan selection screen. Confirm with the board which features they expect before choosing.

<Callout type="tip" title="Don't over-buy on day one">
Communities often start on Starter or Standard and upgrade as they discover features they want. Upgrades are seamless — features unlock immediately. Downgrades are more disruptive (features disappear), so start lower and grow into the higher tier rather than the reverse.
</Callout>

## Setting up billing on a new community

<StepByStep>
<Step title="During community creation">
The onboarding flow asks for plan selection and a payment method. Use your management company's central card or ACH account.
</Step>

<Step title="Verify the billing email">
Set the billing email to your accounting team's address — not a personal admin. Receipts, invoices, and dunning notifications all go there.
</Step>

<Step title="Choose the billing cycle">
Monthly is simplest. Annual is usually 10-15% cheaper but harder to right-size if the community's needs change mid-year.
</Step>
</StepByStep>

## Changing plans

<StepByStep>
<Step title="Open Settings → Billing → Change plan">
From within the community (or your PM dashboard's per-community billing view), open the plan-change flow at `/settings/billing/change-plan`.
</Step>

<Step title="Pick the new plan">
The screen shows what's included and what changes vs. your current plan.
</Step>

<Step title="Review proration">
PropertyPro prorates upgrades on the spot (you pay the difference today) and downgrades at the next renewal (no refund mid-cycle).
</Step>

<Step title="Confirm">
The change takes effect immediately for upgrades; downgrades apply at the next billing cycle so residents don't lose features mid-month without warning.
</Step>
</StepByStep>

## Updating the payment method

<StepByStep>
<Step title="Open the billing portal">
Visit `/billing/portal`. This launches the Stripe-hosted billing portal in a secure popup.
</Step>

<Step title="Add or update a card / bank account">
Manage saved methods, set defaults, and update card-on-file details.
</Step>

<Step title="Verify the change">
Return to PropertyPro. The billing page shows the updated method.
</Step>
</StepByStep>

Multiple methods can be on file; only the default is used for autopay.

## Invoices and receipts

Every billing cycle generates an invoice. Find them at `/billing`:

- **Outstanding**: bills not yet paid.
- **Paid**: receipts for completed payments.
- **Failed**: charges that didn't go through (retried automatically with a dunning sequence).

Each invoice can be downloaded as PDF for your records and tax filings.

## Portfolio-level billing

If your management company centralizes billing across communities:

- **Single payer model** — one card/account on file at the PM level pays for every community in the portfolio. Configure during onboarding.
- **Per-community model** — each community's board sets up their own billing. You see the status across all communities but don't manage payments.

Most PM relationships use the single-payer model with cost pass-through in the management agreement.

## Failed payments and dunning

If a charge fails:

1. **Email notification** — billing email receives an immediate alert.
2. **Automatic retry** — PropertyPro retries 3 days later, then 7 days later.
3. **Service downgrade** — after 14 days unpaid, the community downgrades to Starter (with a 30-day grace period before harder restrictions).
4. **Final notice** — at 30 days, the account enters a frozen state where residents see a banner but data isn't deleted.

Resolve quickly — update the payment method and PropertyPro retries the charge automatically.

## Cancelling a subscription

If a community leaves your management portfolio:

<StepByStep>
<Step title="Coordinate handoff first">
The new management company should be added as admin before you cancel. Otherwise the community loses access.
</Step>

<Step title="Cancel via Settings → Billing">
Pick the cancellation reason (changed management, dissolved, switching tools). The community remains active until the end of the paid period.
</Step>

<Step title="Confirm data retention">
By default, community data is retained for 90 days after cancellation in case the relationship resumes. After that, archive policy applies.
</Step>
</StepByStep>

## Refunds

PropertyPro refunds aren't automatic. To request:

1. Open Help → Contact Management with the invoice number and reason.
2. Support reviews — most refund requests are for billing errors or service issues.
3. Approved refunds process back to the original payment method within 5-10 business days.

## Tax and record-keeping

PropertyPro invoices contain the company name, billing address, and an invoice number suitable for accounting. Your finance team can pull invoices automatically via the Stripe-hosted billing portal.

For tax purposes, PropertyPro subscription fees are usually a deductible business expense for your management company. Pass-through to communities is a separate arrangement governed by your management agreement.

## Common questions

- **"Can a community switch plans monthly?"** Yes, but try not to. Frequent plan changes confuse residents (features appearing and disappearing). Pick the right plan and stick with it.
- **"What if the board wants a feature we're not paying for?"** Upgrade the plan. The cost is typically small relative to the management fee and the feature value to the board.
- **"Does the resident pay PropertyPro directly?"** No — communities are billed at the association level. Residents pay assessments to the association; the association covers PropertyPro from its budget.
