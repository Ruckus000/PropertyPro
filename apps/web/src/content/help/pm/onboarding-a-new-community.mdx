Adding a community to your portfolio is the first thing a new management engagement requires. A clean onboarding sets up everything downstream — compliance, communication, billing — for years.

## Before you start

Have these ready:

- **Community legal name and address** — what appears on the legal records.
- **Community type** — condo (§718), HOA (§720), or apartment.
- **Unit count** — and a roster if available (CSV with unit numbers).
- **Resident roster** — emails of owners and tenants if available (also CSV-able).
- **Board roster** — president, members, term ends.
- **Management agreement** — the signed contract between your company and the association.
- **Plan choice** — which PropertyPro plan tier the community will run on. See [Managing PM subscriptions and billing](/help/pm/managing-pm-subscriptions-and-billing).

If anything's missing, you can still create the community and fill in later — but the more you have upfront, the faster the first week of operations.

## Day 1 — Create the community

<StepByStep>
<Step title="Open PM Dashboard → Add community">
From `/pm/dashboard/communities`, click **Add community** (or visit `/pm/dashboard/communities/new`).
</Step>

<Step title="Enter the basics">
Community name, the subdomain residents will visit (e.g., `sunset-condos.getpropertypro.com`), community type, and unit count.
</Step>

<Step title="Pick the plan">
Choose the plan tier that matches the community's needs and budget. Plan choice drives which features are enabled — `hasCompliance`, `hasMeetings`, `hasFinance`, etc. You can change later, but downgrades restrict feature visibility.
</Step>

<Step title="Set the initial admin">
The first admin is usually the board president or the lead CAM. Enter their email and PropertyPro sends an invitation.
</Step>

<Step title="Submit">
PropertyPro provisions the community within a few minutes — database setup, subdomain DNS, default email templates, FAQ scaffolding. You'll get a confirmation email when ready.
</Step>
</StepByStep>

## Day 1-3 — Initial setup

Once the community is provisioned:

<StepByStep>
<Step title="Configure community details">
Open the community's Settings page. Verify the address, contact info, fiscal year, and any community-specific bylaws references. Adjust default notification cadences.
</Step>

<Step title="Upload governing documents">
Bylaws, declaration, rules, articles. Use [Documents → Upload](/help/documents/uploading-documents) and pick the **Governing Documents** category for each. Set the finalization date to when each document was last adopted.
</Step>

<Step title="Bulk-import units">
For apartment communities, import the unit roster via CSV (see [Managing units and buildings](/help/apartment/managing-units-and-buildings)). Condos and HOAs may not need this if the unit list is small.
</Step>

<Step title="Add the board roster">
Promote board members from the resident list with their correct role (board_president, board_member). Their access takes effect immediately.
</Step>

<Step title="Configure branding">
Apply PM-level branding ([Customizing PM branding](/help/pm/customizing-pm-branding)) or community-specific branding if the board prefers their own look.
</Step>
</StepByStep>

## Day 4-7 — Resident onboarding

<StepByStep>
<Step title="Bulk-invite residents">
Use [Inviting and managing residents](/help/residents/inviting-and-managing-residents) to send invitations. Owners typically come first; tenants follow once their owners are onboarded.
</Step>

<Step title="Set up self-service join">
For communities where you don't have every email, enable the self-service join flow. Residents discover the community via subdomain and submit requests for approval.
</Step>

<Step title="Post a kickoff announcement">
Use [Creating and publishing announcements](/help/announcements/creating-and-publishing-announcements) to introduce PropertyPro to residents. Brief, friendly, explaining what to expect and where to get help.
</Step>

<Step title="Train the local admin">
Walk the CAM or board president through the dashboard for an hour. Cover the compliance dashboard, document library, maintenance triage, and how to publish meeting notices.
</Step>
</StepByStep>

## Week 2 — Compliance and communication baseline

- Review the **compliance dashboard**. Anything overdue from before your management took over? Triage with the board.
- Configure **meeting cadences**. Schedule the next board meeting and set up the notice ahead of statutory windows.
- Set up the **transparency page** (condos and HOAs subject to statutory requirements). See [Configuring the transparency page](/help/transparency/configuring-the-transparency-page).
- Configure **online payments** if the community wants Stripe enabled. See [Setting up online payments](/help/finance/setting-up-online-payments).

## Month 1 — Cadence in place

By the end of the first month:

- Every required statutory document is posted (compliance dashboard at 100% or with Not Applicable explained).
- Recurring assessments are billing.
- Maintenance requests are flowing through Operations.
- Residents know how to log in and find their dashboard.

If anything is still loose, schedule a 30-minute follow-up with the board to align on remaining work.

## Common pitfalls

- **Skipping document upload on day 1** — leads to a 30-day compliance window starting late. Get the basics up immediately even if some documents are still being collected.
- **Wrong plan choice** — choosing a too-low plan locks out features the community needs. Confirm plan and pricing with the board before activating.
- **Roster mismatches** — owners and units must match. CSV imports flag mismatches; investigate before going live.
- **Not training the local admin** — a CAM who doesn't know where to find things calls you for everything. Invest the training hour.

## Handoff to local operations

After the first month, the community should be running on autopilot. Your role shifts from setup to oversight — monthly compliance check, portfolio review, response to escalations.

Document the onboarding in your internal records. When the next community comes on, you have a template to follow.

## Templating future onboardings

After several onboardings, develop a per-customer template:

- **Welcome announcement** copy
- **Initial documents list** specific to your management style
- **Training agenda** for the local admin
- **Day-by-day timing** so future kickoffs hit milestones predictably

Templating saves the next CAM coming on board several hours each onboarding.
