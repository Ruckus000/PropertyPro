Architectural Review Committee (ARC) or Architectural Control Committee (ACC) submissions are how residents request permission to make exterior changes — paint, landscaping, structures. Residents submit from their own account, your committee approves or denies in the queue, and the resident is notified automatically.

<Callout type="florida-statute" title="HB 1203 written-reason requirement">
HB 1203 requires that any denial of an architectural request state specific reasons — typically citing the governing document or rule violated. PropertyPro enforces the part it can: **you cannot record a denial without written reasons.** What it cannot check is whether your reasons actually cite the right covenant, so that judgement stays with your committee. The text you enter is sent to the resident and kept with the submission.
</Callout>

## The review queue

Open the ARC submissions queue to see:

- **Title**
- **Project Type**
- **Unit**
- **Status**
- **Submitted**

Click a row to open the detail panel, where you can also record the decision. Deadline tracking and assigned reviewers are not shown in PropertyPro — track those per your bylaws outside the app.

## Reviewing a submission

<StepByStep>
<Step title="Open the submission">
Click a row. The detail panel shows Status, Description, Est. Start Date, Est. Completion, Review Notes (if any), Decided date (if set), and Submitted date.
</Step>

<Step title="Consult the rules">
Cross-check against your architectural guidelines. Many communities keep this document in the library under Governing Documents.
</Step>

<Step title="Mark it under review (optional)">
If the committee needs time, click **Mark under review**. The resident sees the status change, and any notes you have written are saved so you can come back to them.
</Step>

<Step title="Record the decision">
Write your notes, then click **Approve** or **Deny**. A denial requires notes; an approval does not. The resident is notified either way, and the decision is written to the audit trail with who made it and when.
</Step>
</StepByStep>

## Approving with conditions

There is no separate "approve with conditions" state. Put the conditions in the review notes and approve — the notes are sent to the resident and stay attached to the submission, which is what a future owner or an inspector will look for. Conditions may include material or color restrictions, completion deadlines, or post-work inspection requirements.

## Denying a submission

<Callout type="warning" title="Always include specific reasons">
A denial without specific reasons is legally vulnerable. PropertyPro will not let you submit one without notes, but it cannot judge whether the notes are *good*. Good denials cite the specific rule (e.g., "Section 5.2 of the Architectural Guidelines prohibits pitched roofs above 18 feet") and, where possible, suggest a conforming alternative. "Does not meet guidelines" satisfies the form and not the statute.
</Callout>

The notes you enter are delivered to the resident and stored with the submission, so the written record is kept for you.

## Extensions and appeals

If you need more time than your bylaws allow, communicate with the resident about the delay. PropertyPro does not track extension requests or pause review clocks — manage deadlines per your bylaws outside the app.

## What residents see

Residents open **ARC Requests** to submit a change and to track it. They see the status, and they see your review notes as soon as those are saved — including on an approval. A resident can **withdraw** a request up until it is decided; a withdrawn request stays in the record rather than disappearing.

Residents whose account is not linked to a unit cannot submit, because a request has to attach to a specific home. If one tells you the form is unavailable, check their unit association first.

## Record retention

Approved submissions form part of the architectural history of the unit. A future owner may inherit whatever was approved. Keep the submission, plans, and decision in the record permanently — the review queue archives rather than deletes.

## Tips for a smooth queue

- **Schedule a weekly ARC review** to clear the queue regularly.
- **Template good denials** — capture common rule citations in your committee's reference doc.
- **Use photos generously** — approve photos stored on file help resolve "as-built vs. approved" disputes years later.
