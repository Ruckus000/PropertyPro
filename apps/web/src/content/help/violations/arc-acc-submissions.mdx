Architectural Review Committee (ARC) or Architectural Control Committee (ACC) submissions are how residents request permission to make exterior changes — paint, landscaping, structures. PropertyPro routes the request, captures your review, and archives the decision.

<Callout type="florida-statute" title="HB 1203 written-reason requirement">
HB 1203 requires that any denial of an architectural request state specific reasons — typically citing the governing document or rule violated. PropertyPro requires you to enter written reasons on a denial.
</Callout>

## The review queue

Open the ARC submissions queue to see:

- Resident and unit
- Submission summary
- Attached plans, photos, spec sheets
- Deadline — usually 30 days from submission per your bylaws
- Assigned reviewer

## Reviewing a submission

<StepByStep>
<Step title="Open the submission">
Click any row in the queue. The detail page shows the resident's narrative, all attachments, and the review history.
</Step>

<Step title="Request more information (if needed)">
Unclear submission? Use the comment box to ask for specifics — paint color code, fence height, setback from property line. The clock pauses while you're waiting on the resident (if your bylaws permit).
</Step>

<Step title="Consult the rules">
Cross-check against your architectural guidelines. Many communities keep this document in the library under Governing Documents.
</Step>

<Step title="Vote or recommend">
If you're a committee member, cast your vote. Approval requires a majority (or whatever quorum your bylaws set).
</Step>

<Step title="Finalize the decision">
The chair or designated admin records the final decision and any conditions — for example, "Approved, conditional on using paint code matching building standard."
</Step>
</StepByStep>

## Approving with conditions

Conditions are binding. They become part of the record and may include:

- Material or color restrictions
- Deadline to complete work
- Inspection or photo-documentation after completion

If the resident violates a condition, the conditional approval can be revoked and the original violation process resumed.

## Denying a submission

<Callout type="warning" title="Always include specific reasons">
A denial without specific reasons is legally vulnerable. PropertyPro requires at least one written reason before you can send a denial. Good denials cite the specific rule (e.g., "Section 5.2 of the Architectural Guidelines prohibits pitched roofs above 18 feet") and, where possible, suggest a conforming alternative.
</Callout>

The resident receives the denial with reasons by email. They may resubmit with changes — each resubmission starts a fresh clock.

## Extensions and appeals

If you need more time than your bylaws allow:

- Communicate with the resident about the delay.
- If your bylaws provide for extensions, log one on the submission record.
- Exceeding the statutory or bylaw window without an agreed extension can expose the board to an auto-approval claim. Don't let these sit.

## Record retention

Approved submissions form part of the architectural history of the unit. A future owner may inherit whatever was approved. Keep the submission, plans, and decision in the record permanently — the review queue archives rather than deletes.

## Tips for a smooth queue

- **Schedule a weekly ARC review** to clear the queue regularly.
- **Template good denials** — capture common rule citations in your committee's reference doc.
- **Use photos generously** — approve photos stored on file help resolve "as-built vs. approved" disputes years later.
