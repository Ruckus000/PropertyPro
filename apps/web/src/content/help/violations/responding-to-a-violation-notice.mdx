A violation notice is a formal communication from your association about something at your unit that conflicts with the rules. Responding quickly — even if you disagree — is usually the right move.

## What the notice includes

A well-formed violation notice tells you:

- **The specific rule** — which bylaw, covenant, or regulation you allegedly violated.
- **What to cure** — what you need to fix, change, or remove.
- **The cure window** — how long you have before the next step (typically 14 days).
- **Possible consequences** — fines, a hearing, or suspension of use rights if not cured.

<Callout type="florida-statute" title="HB 1203 written reasons">
Florida's HB 1203 requires specific written reasons for any violation action. PropertyPro structures notices to meet this requirement — if a notice you receive doesn't cite a rule, flag it to the board.
</Callout>

## Reading your notice in PropertyPro

Click the notice in your email or the Violations card on your dashboard. The page shows the notice text, any attached photos, the cure date, and your options.

## Your options

<StepByStep>
<Step title="Cure the violation">
Fix the issue within the window. Photograph the correction when possible. Use the Comment box on the notice to tell the board you've corrected it.
</Step>

<Step title="Request clarification">
If you don't understand what's being asked, comment on the notice with a specific question. Boards often respond within a few days.
</Step>

<Step title="Dispute the violation">
If you believe the notice is wrong, comment with your explanation and any evidence (photos, bylaw references). Ask for a hearing if you want a formal review.
</Step>

<Step title="Attend the hearing">
If the board schedules a hearing, you (or a representative) can attend and present. Hearings follow the notice requirements in your bylaws — usually 14 days' notice.
</Step>
</StepByStep>

## Common violation types

- **Architectural** — exterior changes made without ARC approval.
- **Nuisance** — noise, pet, or lifestyle complaints that violate community rules.
- **Maintenance / appearance** — unkempt yard, stored items in common areas, broken fixtures.
- **Parking** — unauthorized vehicles, wrong space, commercial vehicles where prohibited.

## Fines

If the cure window passes without action, your association may issue a fine. Fines are typically a flat amount or a per-day escalation defined in your bylaws. A fine is formally noticed separately from the original violation — you'll see a new entry on your Violations page.

## Appeals after a decision

If you disagree with a hearing outcome, your bylaws and Florida statute may provide additional remedies — typically an internal appeal, then mediation or court. PropertyPro captures the notice trail, which is useful evidence at any appeal stage.

## What PropertyPro does not do

PropertyPro is a record system — it logs notices, comments, and statuses. It does not decide violations, set fines, or provide legal advice. The board and your association attorney are the right parties for substantive questions.
