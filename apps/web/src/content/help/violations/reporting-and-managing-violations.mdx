The violations workflow in PropertyPro is the record-keeping surface for rule enforcement: log the issue, notice the resident, and capture the outcome.

<Callout type="florida-statute" title="HB 1203 requires specific written reasons">
Florida's HB 1203 (2024) tightened the content of violation notices. Every notice must cite the specific rule and reason. When you create a violation, choose a category and write a description that cites the specific rule — PropertyPro does not enforce this automatically.
</Callout>

## The inbox

Open `/violations` to see violations for your community. Filter by status and severity, or by date range. Each card shows:

- Unit
- Category
- Severity
- Status
- Date

Status values are **Reported**, **Noticed**, **Hearing Scheduled**, **Fined**, **Resolved**, and **Dismissed**.

## Creating a new violation

<StepByStep>
<Step title="Open Report Violation">
Click Report Violation from the sidebar, or open the Violations page and click **New violation**.
</Step>

<Step title="Identify the unit">
Search by unit number. The selected unit populates ownership info automatically.
</Step>

<Step title="Choose category and describe the violation">
Pick a category from the dropdown (Noise, Parking, Unauthorized Modification, etc.) and write a description of what happened, when, and where. Cite the specific rule or covenant in the description — this is your responsibility per HB 1203. Attach photos — they're your best evidence if the resident contests.
</Step>

<Step title="Submit the record">
Creating the violation sets its status to **Reported**. The resident is not notified yet.
</Step>

<Step title="Send the notice">
Open the violation from the inbox and click **Send Violation Notice**. The resident receives an in-app notification. The notice and its evidence are saved to the violation record.
</Step>
</StepByStep>

## Tracking progress

Each violation shows key dates on its Timeline — Reported, Notice Sent, Hearing Date (if scheduled). Cure windows are not tracked in PropertyPro — manage those per your bylaws and paper notices.

Before deciding next steps:

- **Resident cures** → mark as **Resolved**.
- **Resident ignores** → at your deadline, decide: fine, schedule hearing, or dismiss.

## Fining

From the violation detail view, click **Impose Fine**. Enter the amount and due date. The fine posts a ledger entry immediately and appears on the resident's unit ledger in Payments. Status changes to **Fined**.

<Callout type="warning" title="Stick to the bylaws">
PropertyPro does not enforce fine caps or cooldowns automatically. Your bylaws are the authoritative source for how much, how often, and under what procedure you may fine. If you're unsure, consult your association attorney before sending.
</Callout>

## Hearings

For contested violations, click **Schedule Hearing** on the violation record. Enter the hearing date and location. A hearing-notice PDF is available. PropertyPro does not create a calendar event. After the hearing, mark the violation **Resolved** or **Dismissed**.

## Closing a violation

- **Resolved**: issue resolved.
- **Dismissed**: notice was issued in error or the board withdrew enforcement; include a note explaining.

Closing a violation keeps the record in the system for audit but removes it from the open inbox.

## Audit trail

Every action on a violation — notice sent, status change, fine applied — is timestamped and captured in the compliance audit log. That trail is your defense if a resident later challenges a decision.
