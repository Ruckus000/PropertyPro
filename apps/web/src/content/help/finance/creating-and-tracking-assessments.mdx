The assessments register is where admins create, schedule, and track every charge that hits resident accounts — regular dues, special assessments, and late fees. This is the admin counterpart to the resident-facing Payments view.

<Callout type="warning" title="Authority for assessments">
The board is the legal authority for setting and approving assessments. PropertyPro records what was authorized but does not vote, ratify, or recommend amounts. Always anchor an assessment record to a board action (meeting minute, written consent) so the audit trail is complete.
</Callout>

## Recurring dues

Most communities run a recurring monthly, quarterly, or annual assessment cycle. Set it up once and PropertyPro generates the charges automatically.

<StepByStep>
<Step title="Open Assessments → Recurring schedule">
From the assessments page, click the Recurring tab.
</Step>

<Step title="Define the schedule">
- **Frequency**: monthly / quarterly / annually
- **Amount**: per-unit dollar value (or per-unit-type if your community varies)
- **Due day**: which day of the cycle (e.g., 1st of each month)
- **Grace period**: days after due date before a late fee applies
</Step>

<Step title="Apply to units">
Select all units, a unit-type subset, or specific units. New units added to the community after this point inherit the schedule automatically.
</Step>

<Step title="Activate">
Once activated, PropertyPro queues the next charge and emails residents in advance. Residents can pay online if your community has Stripe connected.
</Step>
</StepByStep>

## Special assessments

Special assessments are one-time charges approved by the board for a specific purpose — a roof project, an insurance shortfall, a SIRS-driven repair.

<StepByStep>
<Step title="Verify board authorization">
Confirm the board has voted to approve the special assessment, with the amount, allocation method, and due date in the meeting minutes.
</Step>

<Step title="Create the assessment">
Open Assessments → **New special assessment**. Enter the title (e.g., "2026 Roof Replacement Special Assessment"), the total amount, and the allocation method.
</Step>

<Step title="Pick the allocation">
- **Equal per unit** — every unit pays the same dollar amount
- **By unit percentage** — each unit's recorded share applies
- **Custom** — manually enter per-unit amounts (use sparingly)
</Step>

<Step title="Set the schedule">
- **Single charge** — entire amount due on one date.
- **Installments** — split into 2-12 payments with their own due dates.
- **Effective date** — when the charge appears on resident accounts.
</Step>

<Step title="Attach supporting documents">
The meeting minute authorizing the assessment, any vendor estimates, the reserve-study reference. These attach to the assessment record for the audit trail.
</Step>

<Step title="Publish">
Residents see the new charge on their Payments page and receive an email with the schedule and purpose.
</Step>
</StepByStep>

## Late fees

Late fees apply automatically if you configured them on the recurring schedule. To apply a one-off late fee or override:

1. Open the relevant assessment row.
2. Click **Apply late fee**.
3. Enter the amount and reason.
4. The fee posts to the resident's balance.

<Callout type="warning" title="Bylaws govern late fee amounts">
PropertyPro does not enforce statutory caps on late fees. Florida statute limits how much can be charged; your bylaws may set further constraints. Verify any late-fee amount against your governing documents before applying.
</Callout>

## The register

The Assessments register lists every assessment with:

- Title and date
- Affected units (count + total amount)
- Collected to date / outstanding balance
- Delinquency count (units past due)
- Linked documents

Sort by outstanding balance to see what's still owed; sort by date to walk the year's billing chronologically.

## Reading collection rates

Each special assessment shows its collection rate over time. A healthy assessment hits ~80% paid by the due date and ~95% within 60 days. Lower rates indicate either:

- A communication problem (residents didn't know about the assessment)
- A genuine affordability problem
- A specific delinquency pattern needing follow-up

Use the per-unit delinquency report to identify residents who need outreach versus those genuinely refusing.

## Refunds and credits

To refund a paid assessment (board decided to reverse, vendor came in under budget):

1. Open the assessment.
2. Click **Issue credit** on each affected unit (or **Refund all**).
3. The credit applies to the resident's balance or processes back to their payment method per their preference.

Credits show on resident statements with a clear description so they understand why their balance moved.

## Reporting

The Reports section under Assessments lets you export:

- Year-to-date collections vs. billed
- Delinquency aging (30/60/90+ days)
- Per-unit assessment history
- Special assessment recap (allocation + collection)

Pull these monthly for the management committee and annually for the board's year-end review.

## Common patterns

- **Mid-year rate change** — increase the recurring amount effective on a specific date; existing prior-month invoices keep the old amount.
- **Move-in proration** — for unit ownership changes, manually prorate the first month's assessment.
- **Forgive a late fee** — when a resident appeals successfully, issue a credit equal to the fee. Don't delete the fee — the audit trail should show what was applied and why it was forgiven.
- **Bad debt write-off** — work with your accountant. PropertyPro keeps the record; the accounting treatment happens in your books.
