The audit trail is an append-only log of every mutation that matters for compliance — documents posted, notices sent, elections run, violations issued. Florida's record-keeping requirements lean on this.

## What's logged

Every action with compliance or security relevance:

- Document uploads, replacements, and category changes
- Meeting notices and minutes posted
- Violations issued, updated, resolved
- Elections created, opened, closed, certified
- Ballot submissions (without revealing ballots)
- Access-plan grants and revocations
- FAQ edits
- Emergency broadcasts sent

Each entry captures: the actor, the resource, the action, timestamps, and enough context to reconstruct what happened.

## Reading the trail

<StepByStep>
<Step title="Open Audit Trail from the sidebar">
Click **Audit Trail** in the sidebar (an admin-only entry). The most recent actions appear first.
</Step>

<Step title="Filter">
Narrow by Action (dropdown), date range (Start Date and End Date), or search by name or unit in the User field.
</Step>

<Step title="Inspect an entry">
Expand **Metadata** to see the metadata recorded with the event.
</Step>
</StepByStep>

## When to consult the trail

- **Compliance review** — satisfy yourself (and the board) that required actions happened on time.
- **Disputed action** — a resident claims they didn't receive a notice; the trail proves whether it was sent.
- **Personnel change** — before offboarding an admin, review their recent actions.
- **Incident investigation** — unusual pattern (mass deletions, after-hours access) warrants a look.

## Retention and immutability

<Callout type="florida-statute" title="Append-only by design">
Audit-log entries cannot be edited or deleted. Florida statute expects associations to preserve records for specific retention windows (seven years for most financial records; permanent for governance records). PropertyPro enforces immutability at the database level so an insider cannot erase evidence.
</Callout>

## Exporting

For official records, annual reviews, or turning over records to a new board:

1. On the audit trail page, set your filters.
2. Click **Export CSV**.
3. The export contains the same fields as the on-screen view in CSV format.

## Limits

- Large date ranges can return many rows — narrow your filters before exporting.
- Document downloads ARE logged; other page views are not.
- PII is captured where necessary — treat the export as sensitive.

## What you shouldn't do

- Don't share raw audit exports outside the board and authorized parties.
- Don't use the audit trail as a surveillance tool on staff or residents — it exists for compliance, not micromanagement.
- Don't edit records based on the trail's content; update the underlying data through proper interfaces, which will themselves be logged.

## Common follow-ups

- **Late document postings** — if the trail shows a pattern, tighten your cadence.
- **Unusual admin activity** — investigate; it may be benign or warrant a password reset.
- **Gaps in records** — Florida statute sets minimums; if the trail shows a gap, document why (system outage, staff change) in the next board minutes.
