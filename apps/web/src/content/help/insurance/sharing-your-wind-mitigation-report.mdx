A wind-mitigation inspection documents how your building is built to resist wind — roof deck attachment, roof-to-wall connections, opening protection. Florida insurers are required to offer discounts for these features, but an individual owner can't inspect the building themselves. When you post the report once, every owner can hand it to their own insurer and ask about credits.

<Callout type="info" title="One upload, every owner">
This is the rare board task where a single action benefits every member directly. Owners can't get this document anywhere else.
</Callout>

## Post the report

1. **Upload the inspection PDF** to your document library (the **Insurance** category is a good home for it).
2. Go to **Insurance** in the sidebar and choose **Add Report**.
3. **Pick the document, form, and dates.** We'll fill in a five-year expiry from the inspection date — adjust it if your inspector says otherwise.

That's it. Owners see the report immediately, with a **Download** button and a **Send to My Insurance Agent** button that opens a pre-written email.

## Which form does my building use?

This trips up a lot of boards, and using the wrong form means the inspection has to be redone.

| Building height | Form |
| --- | --- |
| 1–3 stories | OIR-B1-1802 (the Florida OIR uniform form) |
| 4+ stories | Citizens MIT-BT II or MIT-BT III |

The MIT-BT forms can't be completed by a standard home inspector — you'll need an engineer or architect. If your inspector hands you an OIR-B1-1802 for a ten-story tower, something has gone wrong.

<Callout type="warning" title="The form changed in April 2026">
A revised OIR-B1-1802 took effect April 1, 2026. Record which revision your report uses when you add it, so owners aren't handing their insurer an obsolete form without knowing it.
</Callout>

## When it expires

Wind-mitigation forms are generally accepted for about five years. PropertyPro shows a status on each report — current, expiring, or expired — and emails board admins twice before expiry (about six months out and again about a month out), then once when it lapses.

When you commission a new inspection, add it as a new report. A fresh inspection lets every owner ask their insurer about credits again.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can an owner use the association's report for their own policy?**
Yes. The report describes the building, and owners are entitled to a copy from the association.

**Will this lower our members' premiums?**
It may. Florida law requires insurers to offer wind-mitigation discounts, but the actual credit depends on each owner's policy and insurer. Some HO-6 policies see a small benefit because dwelling coverage is limited. Don't promise members a number.

**What about shutters and impact windows?**
Opening protection is often unit-specific and may not be covered by a building-level report. Owners may need to document their own openings separately with their insurer.

**Does PropertyPro send anything to insurers?**
No. Owners download the report and email their own agent from their own email account. PropertyPro doesn't contact insurers and doesn't provide insurance advice.
