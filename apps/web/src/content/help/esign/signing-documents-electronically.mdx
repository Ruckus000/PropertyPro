When your association sends you a document to sign — a lease, a disclosure, an amendment — PropertyPro handles the signature flow end to end. No third-party account required.

## When you receive a signing request

You'll usually see the request in two places:

- **Email** — with a direct link to the signing page for that specific document.
- **Dashboard** — the **Documents to sign** card shows any open requests.

Click either to land on the document.

## How the signing flow works

<StepByStep>
<Step title="Read the document">
The document opens in your browser. Scroll through every page — the **Sign** button stays disabled until you've viewed the full document.
</Step>

<Step title="Review signer details">
The top of the page shows who's requesting the signature, who else is signing, and the signing order (if any).
</Step>

<Step title="Fill any required fields">
The sender may have added text boxes (initials, date, address). Fill them before signing.
</Step>

<Step title="Sign">
Click **Sign here**. You'll either draw your signature with a mouse / finger or pick a typed signature style. Accept the consumer-disclosure checkbox (required by federal e-signature law) and confirm.
</Step>

<Step title="Submit">
Click **Submit signature**. You'll see a confirmation page and a countersigned PDF is emailed to you immediately.
</Step>
</StepByStep>

<Callout type="tip" title="You can sign on your phone">
The signing experience is mobile-friendly. A finger on a phone screen is often a better signature than a mouse on a laptop.
</Callout>

## Where the signed document ends up

Once all parties have signed:

- You receive an emailed PDF with every signer's marks and a timestamped audit trail.
- Your association keeps a copy in their records.
- The countersigned PDF is also available under Documents on your dashboard (typically under Contracts or a category set by the sender).

## If you need to decline

Some requests include a **Decline** option. Declining stops the workflow; the sender receives a notification and can resend with changes. If you're unsure whether you should sign, decline rather than ignore — declining gives the sender clear feedback.

## Legal effect

<Callout type="florida-statute" title="E-signatures in Florida">
Florida adopts the federal Electronic Signatures in Global and National Commerce Act (E-Sign Act) and the Uniform Electronic Transactions Act. An e-signature from this flow has the same legal effect as a pen signature on paper, provided both parties consented to electronic signing — which PropertyPro captures as part of the workflow.
</Callout>

## Troubleshooting

- **"The link expired"** — signing requests have an expiration. Ask the sender to resend.
- **"Sign button is still disabled"** — make sure you've scrolled through every page, including any attachments.
- **"I signed but my partner hasn't"** — multi-signer requests finalize only when every signer has signed. The sender can see who's outstanding.
