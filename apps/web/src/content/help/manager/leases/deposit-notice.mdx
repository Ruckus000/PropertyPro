Florida requires you to tell residents in writing, within 30 days of receiving a deposit, how and where it is held. PropertyPro sends the notice with the account details from the lease.

<StepByStep>

<Step title="Check the notice status" shot="dep-1">

Open the unit. Under **Current lease**, **Deposit notice (§83.49)** shows **Sent** with a date, **Due by** in amber, or **Overdue since** in red.

</Step>

<Step title="Select Edit lease" shot="dep-2">

It is at the bottom of the unit panel.

</Step>

<Step title="Confirm where the deposit is held" shot="dep-3">

Check **Held in** and the depository’s name and Florida address. Both appear on the notice.

</Step>

<Step title="Choose Send it now" shot="dep-4">

Check **Deposit received on** first. The 30-day deadline counts from that date. These options appear only while the notice is unsent.

</Step>

<Step title="Select Save changes" shot="dep-5">

**Deposit notice (§83.49)** changes to **Sent** with today’s date.

</Step>

</StepByStep>

## Notices sent on paper or from another system

In step 4, choose **Already sent** and enter the **Notice sent on** date. If that date is more than 30 days after the deposit was received, the lease records it as sent late.

<Callout type="florida-statute" title="Florida Statute §83.49">

Deposits must be held in a separate Florida account or covered by a surety bond. A higher deposit at renewal needs a new notice. After move-out you have **15 days** to return the deposit, or **30 days** to send a claim notice by certified mail. See [Record a move-out](help:move-out).

</Callout>
