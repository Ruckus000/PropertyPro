Florida law asks larger associations to keep a website with certain records and notices posted.

<OnlyFor types="condo_718">

<Callout type="florida-statute" title="Condo: Florida Statute §718.111(12)(g)">

Condominium associations with **25 or more units** must post specified official records, and notices of meetings, on a website or app. Records go in an area only owners can reach.

</Callout>

</OnlyFor>

<OnlyFor types="hoa_720">

<Callout type="florida-statute" title="HOA: Florida Statute §720.303">

Associations with **100 or more parcels** must post specified records, and notices of meetings, on a website or app, in an area only parcel owners and staff can reach.

</Callout>

</OnlyFor>

## What goes on your site

- A **Documents** section for your official records. It lists the documents you post from **Documents**.
- A **Meetings** section for meeting notices and agendas.

For the full list of records, and the 30-day posting clock, see [Which records Florida requires you to post](help:posting-requirements).

## How the builder checks it

- The label in the top bar shows whether these sections are on your site and visible. Select it to add or show one.
- **Publish** flags a hidden or missing section before you put changes live.
- The **Documents** tool shows each group of records as **Up to date**, **Out of date**, **Saved, not posted** or **Nothing posted**.

If your association is below the size above, the builder marks these sections as recommended, not required.
