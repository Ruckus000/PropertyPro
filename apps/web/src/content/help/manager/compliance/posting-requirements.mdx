- **Governing documents:** declaration, articles, bylaws, rules.
- **Financial:** annual budget and annual financial report.
- **Meetings:** approved minutes, and meeting notices and agendas, both over a rolling 12 months. Both are checked meeting by meeting from **Meetings**. Condos also post video recordings of virtual meetings and required affidavits.
- **Insurance:** current policies.
- **Contracts:** condos post the list of executory contracts and conflict-of-interest contracts; HOAs post current contracts.
- **Bids** received after bidding closes.
- **Condos only:** the question-and-answer sheet, inspection reports, and the SIRS.

These are requirements, not library categories. A requirement is satisfied only once a record is linked on its row in **Compliance**.

When you upload or link a record, enter the date the association **received or created** it. The posting clock runs from that date, not from the upload. The annual budget, the annual financial report and insurance policies are yearly: a file more than a year old no longer counts, and the row asks for the current one.

<OnlyFor types="condo_718">

<Callout type="florida-statute" title="Condo: Florida Statute §718.111(12)(g)">

Associations with **25+ units** must post specified records within **30 days** of creating or receiving them.

</Callout>

</OnlyFor>

<OnlyFor types="hoa_720">

<Callout type="florida-statute" title="HOA: Florida Statute §720.303">

Associations with **100+ parcels** must post specified records on a website or app, in an area only parcel owners and staff can reach.

</Callout>

<Callout type="info" title="HOAs have no posting deadline in the statute">

§720.303 does not say how soon a record must be posted. PropertyPro still gives HOA records a **30-day** clock from the date received or created, as a reminder to keep the site current. A late record shows **Overdue** on that reminder clock, not because the statute sets one.

</Callout>

</OnlyFor>

<Callout type="tip" title="When unit count changes">

The posting clock follows the unit or parcel count in your community settings. Below 25 units (condo) or 100 parcels (HOA), no record can be overdue or due soon, because the website rule does not apply; the rows stay so you can keep posting. When you reach the threshold, the clocks start. Rows you marked **Not applicable** stay that way, so check each one and select **Mark applicable** where it now applies.

The statute counts units per **condominium**. If your association runs more than one condominium, or has timeshare units, PropertyPro counts the association as a whole and models no timeshare exemption. Ask your attorney which count applies to you.

</Callout>
