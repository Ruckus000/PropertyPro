<StepByStep>

<Step title="Select Create Meeting" shot="mt-create">

It is at the top right of the **Meetings** page.

</Step>

<Step title="Choose the meeting type" shot="mt-type">

In **Meeting Type**, **Board** and **Committee** meetings get a 48-hour notice deadline. **Annual**, **Special** and **Budget** meetings get 14 days.

</Step>

<Step title="Enter the title, times and location" shot="mt-form">

Fill in **Title**, **Start** and **Location**. **End** is optional and defaults to one hour after the start. Times are in the community’s time zone. For a video meeting, put the link and call-in number in **Location** and label it clearly.

</Step>

<Step title="Select Create Meeting" shot="mt-save">

The meeting is added to the calendar, and a meeting notice email goes to members based on their notification settings.

</Step>

</StepByStep>

<Figure shot="mt-deadlines" alt="Meeting details showing Notice post by, Vote docs by and Target post by">

Open the meeting to see its **Compliance deadlines**: **Notice post by**, **Vote docs by** and **Target post by** (for minutes).

</Figure>

## Record that the notice is posted

When an upcoming meeting’s notice is not yet recorded, a strip at the top of **Meetings** names it and its post-by time. After you post the notice and agenda, select **Post notice**, then **Record it as posted**. PropertyPro saves the date and who recorded it; it cannot check the posting itself.

## If the meeting is too soon

</OnlyFor>

<OnlyFor types="condo_718 hoa_720">

If the start time leaves less than the required notice, a warning appears under the times while you fill in the form, and again when you save. You can still save, because the law allows emergency board meetings. If it is not an emergency, choose a later date.

</OnlyFor>

## What PropertyPro does not cover

- Posting the notice on the property, or a **mailing**, if your bylaws require one. **Post notice** only records that you did it.
- Longer notice for **assessment votes** (14 days for board meetings) and **condo elections** (60 days and 14 days). Check these yourself; the meeting type does not change for them.
- Weekends and holidays. The post-by date is counted back exactly and keeps the meeting’s time of day, so a 2:00 PM meeting has a 2:00 PM deadline.

<OnlyFor types="condo_718 hoa_720">

<Callout type="warning" title="When you must re-notice">

Adding an agenda item, adding an assessment vote, or moving the time or place (beyond what your bylaws allow) restarts the notice period. Editing a meeting does not send a new notice email, so create a new meeting with the new date and delete the old one.

</Callout>

</OnlyFor>

<OnlyFor types="condo_718 hoa_720">

<Callout type="florida-statute" title="Florida Statutes §718.112 and §720.303">

For owner meetings, and board meetings where an assessment is voted on, the **agenda is part of the notice**. Your bylaws can require more notice than the statute; use the longer of the two.

</Callout>

</OnlyFor>
