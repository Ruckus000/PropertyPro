When a resident submits a maintenance request, staff move it through triage, decide whether it needs a vendor, and dispatch the work. Work orders are the admin-side artifact that connects the request to the vendor doing the work.

## The Operations inbox

Open Operations from the sidebar (the legacy `/maintenance/inbox` route redirects here). The Requests tab shows everything queued for triage:

- Unit and resident
- Category (Plumbing, Electrical, HVAC, etc.)
- Priority — Urgent / High / Normal / Low
- Submitted timestamp
- Photos and description
- Current status

Sort by priority and submitted date to find what needs attention first. Urgent items always come to the top.

## From request to work order

<StepByStep>
<Step title="Open a request">
Click any row in the inbox. The detail page shows the resident narrative, photos, and any back-and-forth comments.
</Step>

<Step title="Confirm the diagnosis">
Read the resident's description. For ambiguous reports, send a clarifying comment before dispatching ("Can you confirm whether the leak is below or above the sink?"). Avoid sending a vendor on a fishing trip.
</Step>

<Step title="Create a work order">
Click **Convert to work order**. The work order inherits the unit, category, priority, and attachments. Add internal-only notes the resident won't see.
</Step>

<Step title="Assign a vendor or in-house staff">
Pick from your vendor list (HVAC contractor, plumbing service, in-house maintenance). Multi-trade jobs may need more than one assignment — create separate work orders.
</Step>

<Step title="Set the schedule">
Enter the scheduled date and time window. The resident sees the appointment on their request page and gets an email confirmation.
</Step>
</StepByStep>

## Vendor selection

PropertyPro lets you maintain a vendor directory with:

- Trade specialty (plumbing, electrical, HVAC, general)
- Contact info (phone, email)
- Service area
- Insurance certificate (uploaded under Documents → Contracts)
- Notes from past jobs

When you assign, the chosen vendor receives an email with the work order details and access to a vendor-scoped view of the relevant request.

<Callout type="tip" title="Keep insurance current">
Before assigning a vendor, verify their certificate of insurance is on file and current. An uninsured vendor on association property is a liability hole. PropertyPro flags vendors with expired insurance in the directory.
</Callout>

## Priority and SLA

Each priority level has a target response window — your community sets the policy, but a typical pattern is:

- **Urgent**: 4-hour response
- **High**: 24-hour response
- **Normal**: 5-business-day response
- **Low**: scheduled within the month

The Operations dashboard surfaces work orders breaching SLA so nothing slips silently.

## Vendor accountability

After the work order is completed:

<StepByStep>
<Step title="Vendor marks complete with notes">
The vendor records what was done, parts used, and any follow-up needed. Photos of the completed work are ideal.
</Step>

<Step title="Resident confirms">
The resident sees the work-complete update on their request and can confirm or flag remaining issues.
</Step>

<Step title="Invoice and close">
Once the resident confirms, mark the work order closed. The vendor's invoice (if any) gets attached for the audit trail.
</Step>
</StepByStep>

## Reassigning a work order

If the assigned vendor declines or can't fit the schedule:

1. Open the work order.
2. Click **Reassign**.
3. Pick a new vendor. The original assignment closes with a "reassigned" status; the new vendor receives the dispatch.

Keep notes on the reason — chronic decline patterns from a vendor signal it's time to find a replacement.

## Working with the resident

Comments on the request thread are visible to the resident. Internal-only notes (on the work-order side) stay between staff. Use the right surface:

- **Resident-visible comments** — schedule changes, questions about access, completion confirmation.
- **Internal notes** — vendor pricing comparisons, performance concerns, decisions about what to charge back.

## Common patterns

- **Recurring vendor issues** — same vendor, same problem repeating. Demote them to back-up status or replace.
- **Resident escalation** — when a resident calls instead of commenting, log a summary on the work order so the audit trail is complete.
- **Emergency dispatch** — for Urgent items outside business hours, the on-call vendor list (in the directory) takes precedence over standard assignments.

## Reporting

The Operations dashboard's Reports tab summarizes:

- Average resolution time by category
- Vendor scorecard (on-time rate, repeat issues)
- Spend by category and vendor (where invoice data is captured)
- Open ticket aging

Use the weekly summary in your management committee report.
