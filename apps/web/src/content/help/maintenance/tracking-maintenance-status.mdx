Once you submit a request, everything about its progress shows up on the Maintenance page. You don't need to call to check in.

## The request lifecycle

Requests move through these statuses, roughly in order:

- **Submitted** — received, waiting in the triage queue.
- **Acknowledged** — staff have seen it and are assigning it.
- **In progress** — work is underway (in-house or with a vendor).
- **Resolved** — the work is complete from the staff's side.
- **Closed** — final state after confirmation.

You'll get an email on every status change. You can also visit Maintenance at any time to see where your request sits.

## Reading a request page

<StepByStep>
<Step title="Click the request in your list">
The list shows your open and recent-resolved requests. Click any row to open the detail page.
</Step>

<Step title="Review the timeline">
Each status change, comment, and file upload appears on the timeline. Staff updates read as `Staff: ...`; your submissions read as `You: ...`.
</Step>

<Step title="Add a comment">
Use the comment box to add new information — a photo, a new symptom, or a preferred access time.
</Step>

<Step title="Upload more photos">
Additional photos help when conditions change (a small leak becomes a big one). Attach directly to the request.
</Step>
</StepByStep>

## If a resolved request isn't really fixed

Residents don't directly reopen requests in PropertyPro. If staff marked something Resolved but the issue continues:

1. Open the request.
2. Add a comment describing what's still wrong, with photos if helpful.
3. Staff can move the request back to In progress from their admin view after they see your comment.

If you don't hear back within a day or two, send a short follow-up comment so it pops back to the top of the staff queue.

## If you see no updates

<Callout type="tip" title="Silent doesn't mean forgotten">
Some updates happen behind the scenes — a vendor being contacted, parts being ordered. If you haven't seen movement in a few days for a non-emergency request, leave a short comment on the ticket. That pokes the right person without starting a phone chain.
</Callout>

## Urgent vs. routine handling

High- and Urgent-priority requests jump to the top of staff queues. Routine (Normal, Low) items enter a weekly queue and get scheduled when a technician has capacity. Setting priority honestly helps everyone — crying wolf on Urgent delays genuine emergencies.

## What staff see

Staff see every request with the same detail you do, plus their internal notes. Comments you post are visible to any staff member working your community. Comments **to** you from staff arrive via email notification and show on the timeline.
