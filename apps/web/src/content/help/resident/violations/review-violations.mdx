A board seat lets you see and act on every unit's violations, not just your own. Only your community manager can file a violation against another unit. To report one yourself, see **Report a rule violation**.

<StepByStep>

<Step title="Open Violations" shot="vi-inbox">

On your **Dashboard**, find the **Violations** card and select **View All**. Filter by status, severity or a date range. Statuses are **Reported**, **Noticed**, **Hearing Scheduled**, **Fined**, **Resolved** and **Dismissed**.

</Step>

<Step title="Open it and select Send Notice" shot="vi-notice">

Select the violation in the list to open it, then **Send Notice**. Add optional notes and select **Send Violation Notice**. The status changes to **Noticed** and the notice date is recorded. PropertyPro emails and notifies every owner and tenant on the unit’s record. Still deliver the formal written notice your governing documents require.

</Step>

</StepByStep>

## Next steps

The actions offered depend on the status.

- **Noticed:** select **Schedule Hearing**, choose the **Hearing Date** and, optionally, the **Hearing Location**. Or select **Impose Fine**.
- **Hearing Scheduled:** select **Impose Fine**, **Resolve** or **Dismiss**.
- **Fined:** select **Resolve** once it is fixed.
- **Resolve** needs resolution notes and **Dismiss** needs a dismissal reason. Both are final.

**Impose Fine** only appears when fines are turned on for your community. Fines are off by default. To impose one, tick the owners on the **Fining committee** who approved it and confirm the approval. If the hearing is less than **14 days** away, a warning appears.

<Callout type="florida-statute" title="Fines need an independent committee">

Florida law requires a fine to be approved by a committee of at least three members who are not directors, officers or employees of the association, or their relatives (§718.303(3), §720.305(2)). PropertyPro only lets you pick owners without a board seat, and never you; it does not check the committee’s size. Every notice must also state the specific rule and reason; PropertyPro does not check this.

</Callout>
