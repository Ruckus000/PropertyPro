Florida statute specifies both **which** records associations must make available and **when** — usually within 30 days of the record being finalized. PropertyPro links each document category to the specific obligation it satisfies.

<Callout type="florida-statute" title="Condo: § 718.111(12)(g)">
Condo associations with 25 or more units must post certain records on an association-managed website. The statute calls out bylaws, articles, rules, financial records, contracts over a threshold, meeting materials, and more.
</Callout>

<Callout type="florida-statute" title="HOA: § 720.303">
HOAs with 100 or more parcels have parallel requirements. Document categories differ slightly; the compliance dashboard shows only the categories that apply to your community type.
</Callout>

## Categories we track

The compliance engine maps each posted document to one of these statutory categories:

- **Governing documents** — declaration / bylaws / articles of incorporation / rules.
- **Financial records** — annual budget, financial report, reserve study, audit.
- **Meeting materials** — agendas, notices, minutes.
- **Contracts** — any vendor or service contract above the statutory threshold.
- **Insurance** — current certificates of coverage.
- **Elections** — candidate bios, ballots, voting outcomes.
- **Official records summary** — a current index of what records are available.

## How the 30-day window works

<StepByStep>
<Step title="A record is finalized">
A document is approved, signed, or adopted — for example, meeting minutes are approved at the next meeting.
</Step>

<Step title="Upload it promptly">
Upload the file under the correct category in Documents. The 30-day clock starts from the finalization date, not the upload date.
</Step>

<Step title="PropertyPro tracks the clock">
Each posted document is checked against the statutory window. Items approaching 7 days flip the compliance row to Urgent.
</Step>

<Step title="Audit trail is preserved">
Even if you post early, the posting time is saved in the audit log. Late postings are not retroactively "fixed" — they remain visible in the history.
</Step>
</StepByStep>

## What counts as "posted"

In PropertyPro, a document counts as posted the moment it's uploaded **and** assigned to the correct statutory category with its finalization date set. If the file is only uploaded but miscategorized, the compliance engine won't credit it.

<Callout type="warning" title="Category matters">
Uploading a budget under "Other" instead of "Annual Budget" won't satisfy the requirement even though the file is visible to residents. Always double-check the category dropdown.
</Callout>

## Common pitfalls

- **Undated documents**: if no finalization date is set, the engine treats the upload date as the clock start. That can artificially mark an on-time record as late.
- **Superseded records**: replacing an old file keeps the statutory obligation satisfied as long as the current version is available.
- **Unit-count changes**: crossing the 25-unit (condo) or 100-parcel (HOA) threshold flips new categories from Not Applicable to required.

## Where to look

- Open **Documents** to review what's posted today.
- Open **Compliance** to see what's missing or overdue.
- Export the compliance report before each board meeting to keep the board oriented.
