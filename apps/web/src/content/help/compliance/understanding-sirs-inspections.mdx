A Structural Integrity Reserve Study (SIRS) is a Florida-statutorily-required engineering review of a condo building's load-bearing structure and the reserves needed to maintain it. PropertyPro tracks SIRS and milestone inspection requirements on the compliance dashboard and shows their posting status on the transparency page — it does **not** evaluate adequacy.

<Callout type="florida-statute" title="Milestone inspections and SIRS are related but separate">
After the 2021 Surfside collapse, Florida enacted SB 4-D (2022) and follow-on amendments under §553.899 and related provisions.

Florida milestone inspection requirements apply to condominium and cooperative buildings that are three habitable stories or more, subject to statutory exclusions. The default first milestone inspection is due by December 31 of the year the building reaches 30 years of age, measured from the certificate of occupancy, and every 10 years thereafter. A local enforcement agency may require the first milestone inspection by December 31 of the year the building reaches 25 years of age based on local circumstances, including environmental conditions such as proximity to salt water. Once the local enforcement agency issues written notice that a milestone inspection is required, additional statutory notice and completion deadlines apply, including owner-notice obligations and phase-one inspection timing. Associations may also have separate statutory obligations after receiving a phase-one or phase-two milestone inspection report, including owner distribution, conspicuous posting, and website or application publication where applicable.

Structural Integrity Reserve Studies are related but separate. Covered residential condominium associations must complete and update SIRS under §718.112(2)(g); covered residential cooperative associations have parallel requirements under §719.106(1)(k), including transition deadlines and coordination rules under Chapter 719. Do not assume the SIRS deadline is always the same as the milestone inspection deadline.
</Callout>

## Who is required to have one

- **Condo buildings 3+ habitable stories** subject to milestone inspection and SIRS requirements under current Florida law.
- **Cooperative buildings** under §719.106(1)(k) and related Chapter 719 transition rules — consult your attorney for building-specific triggers.
- **HOAs** — consult your attorney about whether SIRS or similar reserve-study requirements apply to your community.

If your building is under three stories, or not yet at the applicable milestone or SIRS deadline, you should still track both requirements so you're not surprised when a deadline approaches.

## Phase 1 vs. Phase 2

<StepByStep>
<Step title="Phase 1 — visual inspection">
A licensed engineer or architect walks the building and reviews load-bearing elements visually. Most buildings pass Phase 1 and the engineer issues a summary report.
</Step>

<Step title="Phase 2 — invasive testing">
Triggered only if Phase 1 identifies a substantial structural deterioration that needs deeper investigation. Phase 2 involves destructive testing (cutting into walls, coring concrete) and is much more disruptive and expensive.
</Step>
</StepByStep>

## What PropertyPro tracks and displays

The compliance dashboard tracks two checklist rows: **Structural / Milestone Inspection Reports** and **Structural Integrity Reserve Study (SIRS)**. The public transparency page does not have a dedicated SIRS section — it shows only their **Posted** or **Not yet posted** status under **Document Posting Status**.

PropertyPro does not assess whether reserves are adequate or whether findings require board action — that's the engineer's and the board's call.

## Reserves vs. inspection

The SIRS combines two things: the engineering inspection **and** a reserve study estimating the funds needed to maintain identified components. The reserve component is what drives assessment planning — if the study identifies $2M of future repairs and your reserves hold $500k, the board will need to plan for special assessments or fund-up over time.

## What PropertyPro does not do

<Callout type="warning" title="Engineering and legal advice are out of scope">
PropertyPro is a record system. It tracks whether a SIRS is on file and surfaces the engineer's findings, but does not evaluate the structural conclusions, the adequacy of reserves, or what assessment levels are appropriate. Those judgments belong to your engineer, your reserve analyst, and your attorney.
</Callout>

## Common questions

- **"Our building is approaching 25 or 30 years. Should we start now?"** Yes — engineers book up, and a local enforcement agency may require the first milestone inspection at 25 years. Schedule well before the statutory deadline so the report is ready on time.
- **"The Phase 1 report flags issues. What now?"** Engage your attorney and any structural specialists the engineer recommends. Phase 2 may be triggered. Communicate transparently with owners; SB 4-D explicitly contemplates this disclosure.
- **"Do we have to share the full report publicly?"** The summary must be public. Full reports may contain sensitive engineering detail; consult your attorney on what to redact.

## Ongoing cadence

Milestone inspections repeat every 10 years after the first required inspection. SIRS has its own update schedule under §718.112(2)(g) for condos and §719.106(1)(k) for cooperatives — including Chapter 719 transition deadlines that may differ from milestone timing. Track both obligations separately on your compliance calendar. Engineers and reserve analysts are not next-day services; mark reminders well in advance.
