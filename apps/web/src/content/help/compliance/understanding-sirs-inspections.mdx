A Structural Integrity Reserve Study (SIRS) is a Florida-statutorily-required engineering review of a condo building's load-bearing structure and the reserves needed to maintain it. PropertyPro tracks SIRS and milestone inspection requirements on the compliance dashboard and shows their posting status on the public transparency page — it does **not** evaluate adequacy.

<Callout type="florida-statute" title="SB 4-D and §718.112(2)(g)">
After the 2021 Surfside collapse, Florida enacted SB 4-D (2022) and follow-on amendments. Condo buildings three stories or taller must complete a milestone inspection by age 30 (local authorities may require earlier), and repeat every ten years. A Structural Integrity Reserve Study is also required on the same cadence. The board must maintain inspection records and make summaries publicly available.
</Callout>

## Who is required to have one

- **Condo buildings 3+ stories tall** that have reached the milestone age (30).
- **Cooperative buildings** under parallel provisions of Chapter 719.
- **HOAs** — consult your attorney about whether SIRS or similar reserve-study requirements apply to your community.

If your building is under three stories, or not yet at the milestone age, you should still track the requirement so you're not surprised when the deadline approaches.

## Phase 1 vs. Phase 2

<StepByStep>
<Step title="Phase 1 — visual inspection">
A licensed engineer or architect walks the building and reviews load-bearing elements visually. Most buildings pass Phase 1 and the engineer issues a summary report.
</Step>

<Step title="Phase 2 — invasive testing">
Triggered only if Phase 1 identifies a substantial structural deterioration that needs deeper investigation. Phase 2 involves destructive testing (cutting into walls, coring concrete) and is much more disruptive and expensive.
</Step>
</StepByStep>

## What PropertyPro tracks and displays

The compliance dashboard tracks two checklist rows: **Structural / Milestone Inspection Reports** and **Structural Integrity Reserve Study (SIRS)**. The public transparency page does not have a dedicated SIRS section — it shows only their **Posted** or **Not yet posted** status under **Document Posting Status**.

PropertyPro does not assess whether reserves are adequate or whether findings require board action — that's the engineer's and the board's call.

## Uploading the inspection summary

<StepByStep>
<Step title="Receive the engineer's report">
The licensed engineer delivers the summary PDF (and any supporting documentation).
</Step>

<Step title="Upload under Inspection Reports">
Open Documents → Upload and pick the **Inspection Reports** category.
</Step>

<Step title="Link on the compliance dashboard">
Open **Compliance** and use **Link Existing** on the SIRS row to attach the uploaded report.
</Step>
</StepByStep>

## Reserves vs. inspection

The SIRS combines two things: the engineering inspection **and** a reserve study estimating the funds needed to maintain identified components. The reserve component is what drives assessment planning — if the study identifies $2M of future repairs and your reserves hold $500k, the board will need to plan for special assessments or fund-up over time.

## What PropertyPro does not do

<Callout type="warning" title="Engineering and legal advice are out of scope">
PropertyPro is a record system. It tracks whether a SIRS is on file and surfaces the engineer's findings, but does not evaluate the structural conclusions, the adequacy of reserves, or what assessment levels are appropriate. Those judgments belong to your engineer, your reserve analyst, and your attorney.
</Callout>

## Common questions

- **"Our building is 28 years old. Should we start now?"** Yes — engineers book up. Schedule the inspection 12-18 months before the deadline so the report is ready on time.
- **"The Phase 1 report flags issues. What now?"** Engage your attorney and any structural specialists the engineer recommends. Phase 2 may be triggered. Communicate transparently with owners; SB 4-D explicitly contemplates this disclosure.
- **"Do we have to share the full report publicly?"** The summary must be public. Full reports may contain sensitive engineering detail; consult your attorney on what to redact.

## Cadence after the first SIRS

After the initial milestone inspection, repeat the inspection every 10 years and the reserve study with the same frequency. Mark calendar reminders well in advance — engineers and reserve analysts are not next-day services.
