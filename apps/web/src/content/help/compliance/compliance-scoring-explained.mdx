Your community's compliance score reflects how well you're meeting the Florida statutory requirements that apply to your community type. A higher score means more obligations are satisfied on time.

<Callout type="florida-statute" title="What the score measures">
The score only measures the posting and record-keeping obligations enforced by Florida statute — primarily §718.111(12)(g) for condos and §720.303 for HOAs. It does not assess the financial health, governance quality, or operational state of your association.
</Callout>

## How the math works

Every applicable statutory requirement counts equally:

- **Satisfied** = full credit.
- **Unsatisfied** = no credit (but no penalty beyond that). On the dashboard, an unsatisfied row shows the **Action needed** status pill — there is no "Unsatisfied" label on screen; "Unsatisfied" is just the underlying status used here to explain the math.
- **Overdue** = no credit **and** counts toward the **Overdue** KPI card at the top of the dashboard.
- **Not applicable** = excluded from the score entirely.

The score is `satisfied ÷ (satisfied + unsatisfied + overdue)`, displayed as a whole-number percentage. Posting late still counts as Satisfied, but the record's timing shows in the audit history so late posting patterns are visible to the board.

## What moves the score

<StepByStep>
<Step title="Post any overdue records">
The fastest score improvement comes from clearing Overdue rows. Each one flips to Satisfied as soon as the required file is attached.
</Step>

<Step title="Link a document to each requirement">
Each requirement names the record it needs (e.g. "Annual Budget" or meeting minutes). Open the requirement on the Compliance dashboard and use **Link Existing** (or **Upload**) to attach the matching file — that link, not the document's library category, is what satisfies the requirement.
</Step>

<Step title="Mark applicability correctly">
If your community truly doesn't meet the threshold for a requirement, mark it Not Applicable. Do not leave it Unsatisfied — it will keep weighing the score down.
</Step>

<Step title="Keep meeting records current">
The score tracks approved meeting **minutes** on a rolling 12-month window — keeping these linked prevents the drift that slowly pulls the score down. Meeting-**notice** timing (14 days before owner meetings, 48 hours for board meetings) is tracked and displayed on the meeting record and the transparency page, but it is **not** part of this score percentage.
</Step>
</StepByStep>

## Timing thresholds

When items need attention, a warning banner appears at the top of the dashboard. Below it, four KPI cards summarize status:

- **Readiness** — your compliance percentage and how many items are satisfied.
- **Posting windows** — count of items due inside 7 days.
- **Overdue** — items past their deadline.
- **Needs board action** — items waiting on board approval or review.

Use the **Action needed** filter on the requirements queue to focus on rows that need work soon. Items with more than 7 days of runway are in the comfort zone; once a deadline falls inside 7 days, the row appears under **Action needed** and increments the **Posting windows** KPI.

## What the score does not cover

- Financial audits and reserve adequacy (see your SIRS filing and CPA).
- Architectural review outcomes (tracked separately in Violations/ARC).
- Member communication frequency.

The score is narrow by design. A 100% score means every applicable requirement has a record linked — late postings still earn credit. That is necessary but not sufficient for a well-run association.

## Sharing with the board

Screenshot the dashboard or step through the Satisfied / Overdue / Unsatisfied buckets live at your next meeting. The audit history on each requirement shows when records were posted, which is what the board typically wants context on.
