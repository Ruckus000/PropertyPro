Move-in and move-out checklists capture the condition of a unit at the beginning and end of a lease. Done well, they prevent most security-deposit disputes.

<FeatureGate feature="apartment communities only">
Move-in/move-out inspection checklists are an apartment-community feature. Condos and HOAs don't track unit condition at this level.
</FeatureGate>

## What a checklist captures

- **Room-by-room condition** — kitchen, living room, bedrooms, bathrooms, entry, exterior
- **Category per room** — walls, floor, ceiling, fixtures, appliances, windows, doors
- **Condition rating** — excellent / good / fair / damaged
- **Photos** — attached to each category with damage
- **Notes** — specific descriptions (e.g., "3-inch tear in living-room carpet near window")
- **Sign-off** — both staff and tenant sign the completed checklist

## Running a move-in inspection

<StepByStep>
<Step title="Schedule before the tenant takes possession">
Ideally, the checklist is completed with the tenant present on move-in day. Avoid doing it later.
</Step>

<Step title="Start a new checklist">
Open Move in/out → **New move-in**. Pick the unit and the tenant.
</Step>

<Step title="Walk the unit room by room">
Go room by room. Tap each category, set the condition, attach photos if there's any damage, add notes.
</Step>

<Step title="Have the tenant sign">
The completed checklist is signed electronically by the tenant and the staff member. Both parties get a copy.
</Step>

<Step title="Archive the record">
The signed checklist attaches to the tenant's lease. It becomes the baseline for the move-out comparison.
</Step>
</StepByStep>

## Move-out inspection

At move-out, the same checklist structure is used for a direct comparison:

<StepByStep>
<Step title="Do it after the tenant has fully vacated">
Empty units are easier to inspect. Ideally, walk within 48 hours of key return.
</Step>

<Step title="Open the prior move-in checklist">
Start a new move-out checklist; PropertyPro pulls the move-in condition for comparison.
</Step>

<Step title="Record current condition">
For each category, record the current state. PropertyPro highlights categories where the condition has dropped.
</Step>

<Step title="Document new damage">
Photos and notes on any damage beyond normal wear. This is what supports security-deposit deductions.
</Step>

<Step title="Generate the condition summary">
The final report shows move-in vs. move-out condition side by side. Use this with the tenant when discussing deposit deductions.
</Step>
</StepByStep>

## Normal wear vs. damage

<Callout type="warning" title="Know the distinction">
Florida law distinguishes normal wear (not deductible) from damage (deductible). Small nail holes, minor carpet wear, and faded paint generally fall on the "wear" side. Large holes, pet stains, and broken fixtures fall on "damage." Train staff to mark conditions honestly — over-claiming damage leads to deposit disputes you'll lose.
</Callout>

## Security deposit handling

<Callout type="florida-statute" title="Chapter 83 deposit timelines">
Under Chapter 83 of Florida Statutes, you have specific windows to return or itemize a deposit after the tenant vacates. Missing the window can result in forfeiting your right to claim any deductions. Consult your attorney for the exact timelines — they depend on whether the deposit is held in an interest-bearing account and whether the tenant gives written notice.
</Callout>

## Good photo practices

- Take **wide shots first** — establishes the whole room.
- **Close-ups of damage** — specific nail holes, stains, scratches.
- **Timestamp is automatic** — PropertyPro records when each photo was taken.
- **Shoot in natural light** where possible — interior lights can hide damage.

## Disputes

If a tenant disputes the move-out findings:

1. Share the side-by-side move-in / move-out comparison.
2. Point to specific photos documenting the damage.
3. If the dispute escalates, the signed checklists and timestamped photos are strong evidence.

## Common mistakes

- **Rushing the move-in inspection** — leads to disputes at move-out because conditions weren't baselined.
- **No photos** — notes without photos are much harder to defend.
- **Unsigned checklists** — never close a lease without both signatures.
- **Different staff** — when possible, the same person does move-in and move-out so consistency is easier.
