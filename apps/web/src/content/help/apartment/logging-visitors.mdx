Visitor logging keeps an auditable record of who entered the community — useful for security investigations, insurance claims, and resolving disputes between residents and their guests.

<FeatureGate feature="apartment communities only">
Visitor logging is part of the apartment concierge toolkit. Condos and HOAs typically don't run centralized guest logs.
</FeatureGate>

## Who gets logged

Every non-resident entering the community:

- **Pre-authorized guests** — residents added them to their guest list in advance
- **Walk-in visitors** — show up unannounced; staff contacts the resident
- **Service personnel** — contractors, delivery beyond packages, cleaning services
- **Denied entry** — visitors who couldn't reach the resident or weren't authorized

## Checking in a visitor

<StepByStep>
<Step title="Open Visitors → New entry">
From the Visitors page, click New entry.
</Step>

<Step title="Search for the resident">
Enter the resident's name or unit. Their pre-authorized guest list (if any) appears.
</Step>

<Step title="Match or add the visitor">
If the visitor is on the resident's list, select them. If not, enter name and optionally ID. Take a photo if community policy requires.
</Step>

<Step title="Contact the resident (for walk-ins)">
For walk-ins, call or message the resident to confirm they're expecting the visitor. Record the resident's response.
</Step>

<Step title="Grant or deny entry">
If confirmed, mark as admitted and note the destination (unit number). If denied, record the reason briefly.
</Step>
</StepByStep>

## Pre-authorized guests

Recurring guests — family members, regular friends, caregivers — can be pre-authorized so check-in is a formality rather than a call. Where this is supported in your community, staff can add the guest to the resident's authorized list during a prior visit, or the resident can ask staff to pre-authorize by name.

Pre-authorization speeds up check-in dramatically, especially for residents with frequent visitors like home health aides or grandparents.

## Denied entry

<Callout type="warning" title="Document every denial">
If you deny entry, log the specific reason — "resident unreachable," "visitor declined to wait," "resident denied authorization." Denials without documentation can look arbitrary later if the visitor complains.
</Callout>

## Denied-visitor list

Communities can maintain a denied-visitor list — individuals who are never to be admitted (domestic incidents, prior trespass, court order):

- Added by admin only
- Checked automatically against every incoming visitor
- Entries are private to staff, not visible to other residents

## Resident view

Residents see their own visitor history:

- Who visited (name)
- When
- Whether they were admitted or denied

They don't see visitors who came for other residents.

## Privacy

Visitor data is sensitive. Policy guidelines:

- **Don't share** visitor history outside staff, the resident, and law-enforcement requests with a warrant
- **Retention** — follow your community's retention policy (typically 12-24 months for routine entries)
- **Incidents** — preserve any visit records tied to an ongoing incident until resolved

## Emergency situations

If a visitor becomes threatening or refuses to leave:

1. Call 911 if there's any safety concern.
2. Log the visitor's details in the Incidents section (not just Visitors) for follow-up.
3. Consider adding them to the denied-visitor list after the fact.

## Staffing patterns

- **Peak hours** — weekends, evenings, holiday periods. Staff accordingly.
- **Off-hours** — if the front desk is unstaffed, consider a keypad or intercom system that logs entry even without staff on site.
- **Events** — for large resident gatherings (holiday parties), pre-authorize en masse or use a temporary guest list tied to the event.
