The unit roster is the structural backbone of an apartment community in PropertyPro. Every lease, work order, package, and assessment ties back to a unit. Keep the roster clean and every downstream workflow becomes easier.

## What a unit record holds

- **Unit number** — the canonical identifier residents and staff use
- **Building** — for multi-building communities, the building this unit belongs to
- **Floor** — useful for sorting and filtering
- **Unit type** — studio / 1BR / 2BR / 3BR / townhome
- **Square footage**
- **Bedrooms and bathrooms**
- **Status** — occupied / vacant / off-market / model
- **Current lease** — when applicable, linked to the lease record
- **Notes** — staff-visible notes about quirks, recent renovations, accessibility features

## Adding units

<StepByStep>
<Step title="Open Units → New unit">
Click the Units link in the sidebar, then **New unit**.
</Step>

<Step title="Enter the unit details">
Unit number is required and must be unique in the community. Building, floor, type, and square footage are recommended but not required.
</Step>

<Step title="Mark initial status">
For a newly-built unit not yet rented, mark Vacant. For an existing tenant being added to PropertyPro, mark Occupied and create a lease record next.
</Step>

<Step title="Save">
The unit appears in the roster immediately. You can now attach a lease, log work orders, or assign packages.
</Step>
</StepByStep>

</Callout>

## Buildings and groupings

For multi-building communities, define each building as its own grouping:

- **Building name** — what residents and staff call it ("Tower A", "Garden Building", "117 Main St").
- **Address** — useful when buildings have separate mail addresses.
- **Floor count** — enables floor-based filtering across the community.

Buildings let you target announcements, maintenance routing, and visitor logging at the right population — a flood on the 3rd floor of Tower A only concerns those residents.

## Handling vacancies

When a unit goes vacant:

<StepByStep>
<Step title="Close out the move-out">
Complete the [move-out checklist](/help/apartment/move-in-move-out-checklists). Mark the lease terminated and the unit as Vacant.
</Step>

<Step title="Schedule turnover work">
Create work orders for any cleaning, paint, repair, or carpet replacement needed before re-renting.
</Step>

<Step title="Update photos and listing">
If your community uses the unit details for a public listing, update photos and floor-plan info.
</Step>

<Step title="When re-rented, set Occupied">
Create the new lease record; the unit auto-flips to Occupied when the new lease activates.
</Step>
</StepByStep>

## Renumbering and renaming

If you renumber units (for example, post-renovation):

1. Open each affected unit and update the unit number.
2. PropertyPro preserves the historical numbering — old work orders, leases, and packages still attach to the same physical unit.
3. Communicate to residents about the change so they update mail, deliveries, and emergency contacts.

<Callout type="warning" title="Don't delete units">
Even if a unit is removed from rent rolls (combined with a neighbor, converted to amenity space), keep the record marked as Off-market or Decommissioned rather than deleting. The historical data — past tenants, payments, maintenance — is part of the community's record.
</Callout>

## Filtering and search

The Units page supports filtering by:

- Building
- Floor
- Type
- Occupancy status
- Lease status (expiring soon, expired)

Use filters during turnover season to find every unit reaching the end of its lease, or to plan capital projects by building.

## Reports

The Units module surfaces:

- Occupancy rate (overall and by building)
- Average days vacant per unit
- Lease expirations by month (drives renewal outreach)
- Unit-mix analysis (how many of each type)

These metrics inform capital planning, staffing, and marketing efforts.

## Common patterns

- **Adding a new building** — create the building first, then bulk-import the units within it.
- **Combining units** — mark one of the original units as Off-market with a note pointing to the combined unit's number. Keep the historical record intact.
- **Off-market vs. vacant** — Vacant means available to rent. Off-market means temporarily unavailable (under renovation, model unit, amenity space). The distinction matters for occupancy reporting.

## Tenant linkage

Each tenant on a lease is linked to a unit. When you offboard a tenant:

- Their access to the unit ends with the lease termination.
- The unit reverts to Vacant if no other lease is active.
- The tenant's PropertyPro account stays (they can keep using it for other communities).

This separation — units are owned by the community, accounts are owned by individuals — keeps the audit trail correct as residents move in and out.
