Creating a meeting notice is the admin-side flow for putting a compliant notice into the community record. Get the timing and posting channels right and the compliance dashboard reflects success.

## Pick the right meeting type

PropertyPro offers five meeting types when you create a meeting:

- **Board** — PropertyPro calculates a 48-hour notice window
- **Annual** — 14-day notice window
- **Special** — 14-day notice window
- **Budget** — 14-day notice window
- **Committee** — 48-hour notice window

Florida statute may require longer notice for assessment votes or elections than the type you select here. If your meeting includes an assessment vote or election step, verify the statutory window manually — PropertyPro does not distinguish those cases in the form.

## Creating the notice

<StepByStep>
<Step title="Open Meetings → Create Meeting">
Click Meetings, then **Create Meeting** in the top right.
</Step>

<Step title="Fill in Title, Meeting Type, Start, End, and Location">
Enter a title, pick the meeting type, set start and end times, and add the location or call-in details. Virtual-only meetings must be clearly labeled and accessible per your bylaws.
</Step>

<Step title="Click Create Meeting">
Click **Create Meeting** to save. Compliance deadlines (**Notice post by**, **Vote docs by**, **Target post by**) appear on the meeting detail modal after the meeting is created.
</Step>
</StepByStep>

## Posting channels

A notice is considered posted when:

1. Published in PropertyPro (automatic)
2. Delivered to members per bylaws (usually email — PropertyPro handles this)
3. Physically posted on a community bulletin board, if required

PropertyPro covers #1 and #2. You're responsible for #3.

## Agenda requirements

<Callout type="florida-statute" title="Agenda is part of the notice">
For owner meetings and for board meetings where an assessment is voted on, the agenda is part of the statutory notice. Adding an agenda item after the notice goes out requires re-noticing.
</Callout>

## Re-noticing

Any of these require a fresh notice with a new window:

- New agenda item added
- Assessment vote added to a notice that didn't include one
- Time or location change beyond what your bylaws permit

## After the meeting

- Approve minutes at the next meeting.
- Post approved minutes under Documents → **Meeting Records** (within 30 days — tracked on compliance).

## Common mistakes

- **Posting the notice but forgetting to include the agenda**
- **Scheduling a board vote on an assessment without 14-day notice**
- **Changing the meeting time hours before the meeting** — short moves generally require re-notice

Be conservative on timing — the cost of over-noticing is zero; the cost of an invalid meeting is substantial.
