Creating a meeting notice is the admin-side flow for putting a compliant notice into the community record. Get the timing, the agenda, and the posting channels right and the compliance dashboard reflects success.

## Pick the right meeting type

- **Board meeting (routine)** — 48 hours
- **Board meeting with assessment vote** — 14 days
- **Annual owner meeting** — 14 days
- **Special owner meeting** — 14 days
- **Election — first notice** — 60 days
- **Election — second notice** — 14 days

The meeting type drives the earliest compliant post date, which PropertyPro pre-fills.

## Creating the notice

<StepByStep>
<Step title="Open Meetings → New Meeting">
Click Meetings, then **New meeting** in the top right.
</Step>

<Step title="Select type and date">
Pick the meeting type and date/time. PropertyPro computes the minimum notice posting date based on the type.
</Step>

<Step title="Enter location or call-in">
Physical address, virtual link, or both. Virtual-only meetings must be clearly labeled and accessible per your bylaws.
</Step>

<Step title="Attach the agenda">
For owner meetings and any board meeting voting on an assessment, the agenda is required at notice time. Upload the agenda PDF or draft inline.
</Step>

<Step title="Review posting status">
The preview shows whether the notice is within the required window. If posting late, PropertyPro flags it — you can still post, but the late flag is visible in the record.
</Step>

<Step title="Publish">
Publish. The notice goes into Meetings, emails fan out by resident preference, and the compliance dashboard refreshes.
</Step>
</StepByStep>

## Posting channels

A notice is considered posted when:

1. Published in PropertyPro (automatic)
2. Delivered to members per bylaws (usually email — PropertyPro handles this)
3. Physically posted on a community bulletin board, if required

PropertyPro covers #1 and #2. You're responsible for #3.

## Agenda requirements

<Callout type="florida-statute" title="Agenda is part of the notice">
For owner meetings and for board meetings where an assessment is voted on, the agenda is part of the statutory notice. Adding an agenda item after the notice goes out requires re-noticing.
</Callout>

## Re-noticing

Any of these require a fresh notice with a new window:

- New agenda item added
- Assessment vote added to a notice that didn't include one
- Time or location change beyond what your bylaws permit

## After the meeting

- Approve minutes at the next meeting.
- Post approved minutes under Documents → Meeting Minutes (within 30 days — tracked on compliance).
- Update the meeting record to Past if not updated automatically.

## Common mistakes

- **Posting the notice but forgetting to include the agenda**
- **Scheduling a board vote on an assessment without 14-day notice**
- **Changing the meeting time hours before the meeting** — short moves generally require re-notice

Be conservative on timing — the cost of over-noticing is zero; the cost of an invalid meeting is substantial.
