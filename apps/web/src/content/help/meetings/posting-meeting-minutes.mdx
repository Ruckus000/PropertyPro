Meeting minutes are official records under §718.111. For condominium associations subject to §718.111(12)(g), the website-posting subsection specifically lists **approved** board minutes — posting them promptly helps satisfy that obligation.

<Callout type="florida-statute" title="§718.111(12)(g) — website posting of approved minutes">
For condominium associations subject to §718.111(12)(g), approved board minutes are among the records that must be posted or made available through the association's website or application. Unless a shorter deadline applies, records required to be posted under the website/application subsection must be made available within 30 days after the association receives or creates the listed official record. Because the website-posting subsection refers to approved board minutes, the statutory website-posting deadline should not be described as automatically running from the meeting date.

PropertyPro displays a **Target post by** label on the meeting detail modal — 30 days after the meeting date — as a conservative internal reminder based on meeting date. Hover the label for a disclaimer: statutory website-posting deadlines may depend on when approved minutes are created or received. Confirm the legal website-posting deadline with counsel.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="Video meeting recordings">
§718.111(12)(g) separately requires video-conference meeting recordings to be maintained and posted or hyperlinked for the preceding 12 months. If your association holds video meetings, posting approved minutes and posting recordings may involve separate obligations — consult your attorney.
</Callout>

## The approval-to-posting flow

<StepByStep>
<Step title="Prepare draft minutes">
The secretary or designated admin drafts the minutes after the meeting. Keep them factual — decisions, votes, attendance, time.
</Step>

<Step title="Approve at the next meeting">
At the subsequent board meeting, the board votes to approve the minutes (often with edits).
</Step>

<Step title="Upload to Documents">
Open Documents, click **Upload Document**, and pick the **Meeting Records** category.
</Step>

<Step title="Link to the meeting">
Open the meeting on the calendar and click **Author Minutes** to write minutes linked to the meeting.
</Step>
</StepByStep>

## What minutes should include

- Meeting type, date, time, location
- Members present and absent
- Whether quorum was met
- Every motion, who moved/seconded, and the vote result
- A summary of discussion (not a transcript)
- Adjournment time

## What they shouldn't include

- Detailed personal information (medical, HR disputes) — redact or summarize
- Legally privileged discussions (attorney-client consultations happen in executive session and aren't in public minutes)
- Opinions of individual members unless part of an official statement

## Late posting

If you miss the statutory or internal posting window, still post — late posting is better than never. PropertyPro may show a late-posting flag on the compliance audit history once the in-app target passes, but the requirement moves from Overdue to Satisfied once approved minutes are on file. Confirm with counsel whether late posting satisfies the statutory obligation in your circumstances.

## Minutes vs. transcripts

Minutes are a summary, not a transcript. Do not record meetings and post the recording as "minutes." A recording may be a supplement, but it does not replace formal minutes because:

- It's much harder for residents to scan
- It complicates redaction of sensitive information
- Florida statute expects a written record

## Correcting posted minutes

If the minutes were approved and later found to have an error:

1. Discuss at the next meeting.
2. Move and vote to approve a corrected version.
3. Delete the old file and re-upload the corrected version, or author a corrected version through **Author Minutes**.
4. Keep a note in the meeting record about the correction.

Residents see the current version; the audit trail preserves the original.
