Meeting notices are one of the most scrutinized posting obligations under Florida condo and HOA statutes. PropertyPro tracks the timing and flags notices that slip.

<Callout type="florida-statute" title="Why the timing is prescribed">
Florida statute requires condo and HOA boards to give specific advance notice for different meeting types. The statute sets the minimum window — your bylaws may set longer.
</Callout>

## Timing at a glance

- **Board meeting (general)** — 48 hours
- **Board meeting with assessment decision** — 14 days
- **Annual owner meeting** — 14 days
- **Special owner meeting** — 14 days
- **Election meeting (condo)** — two-step: 60 days / 14 days
- **Hearings (violations, ARC)** — 14 days (per bylaws)

The shorter window (48 hours) applies to routine board meetings where no new assessment is voted on. The 14-day windows apply to anything member-facing. Your specific bylaws may impose stricter requirements.

<Callout type="info" title="How PropertyPro calculates notice windows">
PropertyPro offers five meeting types and applies a 48-hour notice window to **Board** and **Committee** meetings, and a 14-day window to **Annual**, **Special**, and **Budget** meetings. It does not have separate types for assessment votes or elections — if your meeting falls into one of those categories, verify the statutory window manually.
</Callout>

## What counts as "posted"

Posting requires all of the following:

- Notice is published in the PropertyPro meeting list **and**
- Mailed or emailed to members per your bylaws **and**
- Posted physically in the common area if the community uses a bulletin board

PropertyPro handles the first automatically — creating a meeting queues a notice email to members. The physical posting is your responsibility.

### Deadlines can fall on a weekend

The post-by date PropertyPro shows you is exactly the statutory window counted back from the meeting — 14 days, or 48 hours for a board meeting. Florida law grants no weekend or holiday exception, so that date can land on a Saturday or Sunday, and the deadline carries the meeting's time of day: a 2:00 PM meeting has a 2:00 PM post-by, not end of day.

The app posts electronically, so a weekend deadline costs it nothing. If your bylaws also require **mailing** or a **bulletin-board posting**, plan those around it yourself — schedule the meeting so the deadline falls on a business day, or complete the mailing early. PropertyPro will not quietly move the date to the previous Friday for you, because that would be telling you the statute says something it does not.

## When you have to re-notice

Any of these changes restart the notice window:

- New agenda item added after the notice went out
- Assessment added to a board meeting that was noticed as a general board meeting
- Time or location change (unless you're within the allowed rescheduling window in your bylaws)

<Callout type="warning" title="Don't paper over a missed notice">
If you discover a meeting was under-noticed, the cleanest remedy is to reschedule with a fresh, compliant notice. Holding the meeting anyway exposes the board to a challenge on any action it takes.
</Callout>

## After the meeting

- Approve minutes at the next meeting.
- Post approved minutes within the 30-day window (tracked on the compliance dashboard).
- Retain the notice and any acknowledgements in the official records.

## Bylaws vs. statute

The statute is the floor. Your bylaws can set stricter rules (longer notice, additional posting channels). PropertyPro doesn't know your bylaw details — when the bylaws differ from the statute, configure the posting window with the longer of the two.
